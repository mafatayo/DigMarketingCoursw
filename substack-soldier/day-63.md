*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 63 — Dispatch: Carols on the Radio

On the holiday night, Vira did something unauthorized, and I'm glad she did.

It was after midnight. The table had been cleared, the dishes washed, the candle burned to a puddle. Most of us were half asleep, wrapped in blankets, in that warm swimmy state that follows a good meal and a long day. Vira, at the radio as always, was keeping the net alive with the quiet, patient clicks of a woman on duty.

And then she did a strange thing. She turned the volume up on the open channel, keyed the mic, and said, in a low voice, "Attention all posts on the net. This is Magpie. If anyone's awake, I have something for you."

A pause. Static. Then a handful of answers, drowsy, curious. "Go ahead, Magpie." "Magpie, we're listening."

She cleared her throat. I saw her glance round the dugout, almost shy. Then she began to sing.

It was a carol. The old one, the one every child in the country learns. A simple tune, climbing slowly, with a line that falls like a snowflake. She sang it unaccompanied, in her low, rough, true voice, into the microphone, the words floating out across the black frozen fields.

I stopped breathing. Halyna sat up. Bohdan opened his eyes.

By the second verse, the net had changed. Someone from the next position had joined in, faintly, a man's voice, cracked and sweet. Then another. A woman, further off, with a lovely high note. Then a whole choir, thin and scattered and astonishing, voices of strangers coming through the static from a dozen dugouts, across miles of snow, none of them seeing each other, all of them singing the same song.

Motor sang. Zenko sang. Yaroslav, his voice cracking, sang. I sang, which I never do, with my head down, and I couldn't hear myself, and it didn't matter.

When the carol ended, a hush. A long crackling silence, full of breath. Then a voice, an older man, hoarse and warm, from somewhere to the north: "Thank you, Magpie. Merry Christmas to all."

"Merry Christmas," came back from the dark, in a dozen accents. "And to you. And to you."

Vira let go of the mic. Her hands were shaking. She looked at me across the dugout, her face wet. "That," she said, "was extremely against regulations."

"I know."

"I'll do it again next year."

"I know," I said.

# Day 63 — Notes: Songs That Carry

Things I'd say about a voice in the dark.

1. **A song doesn't need a stage.** It needs one person willing to begin.
2. **Voices find each other.** You start, and somewhere, someone answers.
3. **Old songs hold more.** A carol has a thousand winters in it.
4. **The worst singer in the room is often the loudest. Let them.**
5. **After a song, silence is different.** Fuller.
6. **Don't ask permission.** (Within reason. Don't tell Motor I said that.)

**What I'm grateful for:** A tired radio operator with a very good alto.

What's a song you could start in the dark, and who'd answer?
