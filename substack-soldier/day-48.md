*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 48 — Dispatch: A Bad Day

I'm not going to tell you what happened.

That's a decision I made this morning, and I want to explain it, because you've followed me for forty-eight days and you deserve a reason.

It was a bad day. One of the worst. Something happened that the others and I have agreed, without a word, not to describe in detail. Not because it's secret. Because it isn't ours to hand around. There's a man whose family may read this. There are things that belong to the people they happened to.

So here is what I can say.

The day started like any other. Cold. Gray. Tea. Then, around ten, the day split. A before and an after, with a thin, sharp seam through the middle. And everything afterward moved very slowly, as if underwater. I remember specific, useless things. The click of a boot on frozen mud. The flat white of the sky. Halyna's voice, steady, saying a name. The weight of something I was carrying. The sound of Vira, very quietly, being sick behind a sandbag, and nobody looking.

I remember Motor standing very still with his cap in his hand.

I remember the Colonel, who didn't bark. He walked to the spot, sat, and stayed there, in the cold, for an hour, as if keeping a watch.

Afterwards we sat in the dugout, the six of us, and for a long time nobody spoke. The kettle was cold. Nobody lit the stove. Somebody had put a cup in front of me and I hadn't touched it, and the tea had grown a skin.

Zenko was the first to move. He got up, very slowly, took off his glasses, wiped them on his scarf, and put them back on. "Gentlemen," he said, and his voice broke on it. "Ladies. I would like to say something. And I find I can't. So let us just sit."

We sat.

It was, I think, the most honest hour of my life. Nobody offered comfort. Nobody said "it will be all right." Nobody pretended. We just sat together in a hole in the earth and let the day be what it was.

At some point Halyna reached across and took my hand. Then Vira took Halyna's. Then Bohdan took Vira's. A ring of cold, clumsy hands around the table, no one speaking. I felt the small bones in each one. The warmth, such as it was.

Tomorrow I'll write about something else. Tonight I only want you to know this: if I go quiet sometimes, it's because I'm carrying something that isn't mine to put down.

# Day 48 — Notes: Why I Don't Write About It

Some honest rules I've set for myself.

1. **I don't write what belongs to someone else's family.**
2. **I don't write for shock.** There's enough of that.
3. **I don't write names without permission.**
4. **I don't write it for pity.** Please don't send pity.
5. **I do write that it happens.** Silence would be a lie, too.
6. **I do write what the rest of us do after.** That's the part worth knowing.

**What helps afterward:**
- Sitting together.
- Tea, even cold.
- A task in the morning.
- Someone saying the name.

I'll be back tomorrow. Thank you for understanding.
