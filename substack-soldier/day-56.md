# Day 56 — Dispatch: Hot Tea at Dawn

The best hour of my day is 5:40 a.m., and I'd like to defend that.

It's not the sleep I miss, though I'm always short. It's the pause. By 5:40 the night watch is ending, the day watch hasn't started, and a gap opens in the clock, thin as a crack in a door. Everyone's half awake. Nobody's yet had to be anything.

I get up before the others, most days. It isn't virtue. My back wakes me. I lie there listening to the dugout breathe: Motor's slow snore, Vira's tiny whistle, the click of the stove cooling. Then I pull on my boots, careful not to wake the Colonel, who sleeps on my feet like a hot water bottle with opinions, and I take the kettle outside.

The cold at that hour is a different cold. Cleaner. It smells of iron and frost and woodsmoke from a stove somewhere. The sky is the color of a dove's chest, gray going to pink along one edge. The field is silver. Every blade of grass has its own white fur.

I light the burner behind the sandbags, shielding it with my body, and wait for the kettle. It takes a while. The metal ticks. A small blue flame hisses. I hold my hands over it, the way you do over a campfire, and my fingers slowly return to me.

When the water boils I make the first tea. Two bags in the big pot, a spoon of sugar, a slice of something dry from the shelf. I pour a mug for myself and stand there, with the steam on my face, looking east.

This is the part I want to hold. The first sip. The way the heat slides down and spreads in the chest like a small sun. The birds start up. Not many, a handful, tentative, tuning. Somewhere far off a dog barks once and thinks better of it.

Sometimes a figure comes up the steps behind me. Usually Halyna. She never speaks. She stands next to me and I pour her a mug. We drink together, watching the light, saying nothing. It's the most companionable silence I know.

Today it was Motor. He came up, hands in pockets, mustache frosted. I handed him tea. He drank, grunted, and after a minute said, "Good tea."

"Thank you."

"I'll hum about it."

"Please do."

And he did, softly, the four bars drifting out over the frozen field. The sun came up while we stood there. Pink, then gold, then white. And the day, as it does, began.

# Day 56 — Notes: Dawn Rituals

How I start, in order.

1. **Boots, quietly.**
2. **A word to the dog.** "Good morning, Colonel." He ignores it.
3. **Kettle out.** Always the same spot.
4. **The first look.** At the sky. Not the field. The sky.
5. **The first sip.** Nothing else until it's done.
6. **One thought of home.** Katya, Sonya, in that order, then the mother in the hospital.
7. **One small intention.** "Today I'll be kind." Or "Today I'll finish the watch." Something I can keep.
8. **Then the day.**

**Why it matters:** The first hour sets the tone. If it's chaos, the whole day is chaos. If it's tea, it's tea.
