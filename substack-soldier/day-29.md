# Day 29 — Dispatch: A Letter from a Stranger

I want to tell you about a letter I received that wasn't for me.

It came in a bundle of cards from a school abroad. Hundreds of them. Children's cards, mostly, in many languages, glittering with glue and glitter. Vira sorted them on the table, trying to share them equally. Mine was a folded piece of blue paper with a lopsided heart on the front.

Inside, in careful, uneven English: *Dear soldier. I am Maria. I am eight. I live in a small town in another country. My teacher told us about your country. I am sorry about the war. I drew you a flower. I hope you are warm. I hope you have a dog. Love, Maria.*

Under the words, a drawing. A big orange flower with a face. A dog, drawn as a circle with legs and a long smiling mouth. And next to the dog, a stick figure in a hat, labeled, in small letters, "YOU."

I read it twice. Then a third time. I don't speak English well, only enough to follow movies, and the grammar was shaky, but it didn't matter. I understood every word.

There's something about getting a letter from a stranger who owes you nothing. It's different from a letter from home, which carries love and worry and the weight of history. A stranger's letter is lighter. It says only: *someone out there thought of you.* A thin, clear message. It went through me like a drink of water.

I showed it to the Colonel. I know that's absurd. I held it up and said, "Look, she drew you." He sniffed it, thoroughly, with the air of a man inspecting a contract, then licked the flower. I decided that counted as a reply.

That night I wrote back. In bad English, with Bohdan checking the spelling. "Dear Maria. Thank you for your letter. I am warm. I have a dog. His name is Colonel. He is very important. Your flower is on my wall. Please be kind to everyone. Please play outside. The sun is good. Love, Lys." Then I drew a fox beside the dog.

I thought about it all night. Maria will never know what that letter did. She'll go to school. She'll forget. She'll grow up and be a woman in a city I'll never see, and somewhere in a pocket of a man in a hole in the ground, a blue heart will still be folded.

That's the strange arithmetic of kindness. You send a small thing out and have no idea where it lands.

# Day 29 — Notes: Why Words from Abroad Matter

I've been thinking about why it hits so hard.

1. **They didn't have to.** There's no obligation. That makes it a gift.
2. **They see us.** It's easy to feel erased in a long war. A letter says: you exist.
3. **They are the future.** Children writing to soldiers are learning something about the world.
4. **They break the loneliness.** At the front, you can feel like the world has moved on. A card from far away says: not entirely.
5. **They're proof.** That people are decent in the plainest, weirdest places.

**If you'd like to write:**
- Keep it short.
- Be kind, not pitying.
- Ask a question. We love questions.
- Include a drawing, even a bad one.
- Don't ask about the war. Ask about the dog.

Every card goes on the wall. We have a wall now.

If you've ever written a letter to a stranger, tell me what you said.
