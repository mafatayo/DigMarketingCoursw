# Day 84 — Dispatch: Letters to the Future

Zenko had an idea, and for once it wasn't about Plutarch.

"We should write to the children," he said. "Not our own. The ones who will come after. The ones who will go to school in the buildings we've drawn on the wall."

"What would we say?" said Vira.

"The truth. Gently."

It took us a week. Each of us, one by one, in the quiet hours, took a sheet of paper and a pencil and tried. Some didn't manage. Bohdan wrote four words and tore it up. Yaroslav wrote an entire page, then burned it in the stove, then wrote another. Halyna, with a nurse's pragmatism, produced a short list, and then crossed through it and wrote only one sentence.

I'll share mine, with the others' permission to share theirs. A little collection. We've put them in a tin, an old biscuit tin with a dented lid, and Zenko will keep it. "I'll open it," he said, "when we win. In a classroom."

*From Halyna:* "Wash your hands. Be kind to nurses. Drink water. Everything else is detail."

*From Vira:* "Learn to listen. It's the most useful thing I know. And laugh. Loudly. Someone will thank you."

*From Bohdan:* "Make things. Fix things. Don't be afraid of a small broken machine. Be kind to it. Be kinder to the people who built it."

*From Yaroslav:* "Look after the animals. They didn't ask for any of this."

*From Motor, in blocky capital letters:* "BE ON TIME. HELP WITH THE BUCKETS. SING."

*From Maksym:* "Ask questions. Always. It's the bravest thing you'll do."

*From Zenko, in a long, careful, beautiful hand:* "You will be told that history is a list of dates. It is not. History is a very large number of tired people doing small decent things, mostly unobserved. You are the next ones. Do likewise."

And mine:

*Dear child,*

*I don't know your name, or your face, or whether you like sunflower seeds. But I know that you're reading this in a room with a window, and that there is no siren.*

*I'd like to tell you what the world sounded like in the year I wrote this. It sounded like a kettle, and a stove, and a man humming four bars of a song about a bus. It sounded like a carol, over a radio, in the dark, sung by a hundred strangers. It sounded like a lark, going up.*

*Listen. There's always something under the first sound. Listen under it.*

*Be gentle. It's very easy. It's also very rare.*

*With love from the past,*

*Lys.*

We sealed the tin with a strip of pink duct tape. Zenko placed it on the highest shelf, beside the cat mug.
