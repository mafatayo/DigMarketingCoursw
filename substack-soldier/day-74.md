*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 74 — Dispatch: Tired of Headlines

Zenko asked me something strange at dinner. "When did you last see a report that made you feel seen?"

I thought about it. "I can't remember."

"Neither can I." He pushed a bean across his plate. "I'm starting to think we've become weather."

I knew what he meant. For months now, coverage of the war has thinned. The long front-page pieces have shrunk to short columns, then to a line, then to a ticker at the bottom of a screen. People who once checked the news hourly now check it weekly. We've become a kind of background noise, like traffic, a thing that's always there and sometimes bad, and mostly unremarked.

I don't blame them. I mean that. A person can only feel so much, and for so long. If I lived at home, I'd probably tire too. There's a limit to how much heartbreak the human body can hold. The world has its own wars, its own troubles, its own bills.

But from here it feels strange. A little like being a man waving from a distant ship. You see the faces on the shore, getting smaller, and think: *surely they'll look up again.*

Vira was fierce on the subject. "I don't need to be famous," she said. "I need to be *remembered.* There's a difference."

"What's the difference?" said Yaroslav.

"If you're famous, you're a story. If you're remembered, you're a person."

"I'd take remembered," said Halyna.

"I'd take dry boots," said Motor.

We laughed, but it stayed with me. In the weeks since, I've found myself watching more closely the way the world looks at us. The glance that slides, the headline scrolled past. And I've realized it isn't cruelty. It's exhaustion, or distance, or a fear of caring too much.

So I've made myself a rule. I won't be angry at the tired. I'll just keep telling the small true things. A dog. A mug. A carol over the radio. If one person, somewhere, stops scrolling for one minute and thinks *oh, they're still there,* I count it as a victory.

You're here. That's the proof. Thank you.

# Day 74 — Notes: On Being Forgotten

What I'd say, for what it's worth.

**To the tired:** It's okay. You don't have to feel it all, all the time. Do one thing.

**To the people who keep reading:** You matter more than you know.

**To anyone who feels forgotten, in any war, any illness, any lonely room:** You exist. Someone out there is thinking of you right now. Even if they've never met you.

**One small habit:** Once a week, pick one thing. A letter, a donation, a call, a share. Keep it small. Keep it going.

Don't look away. Don't drown either.
