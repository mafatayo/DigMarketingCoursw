# Day 66 — Dispatch: Leave, Day One

The third stair still creaks.

That was the first thing. I'd planned to be dignified. I'd rehearsed, on the train, a calm, strong entrance, shoulders back, a steady voice. Instead I climbed four flights with my bag banging against my knees, stopped on the landing to catch my breath, and put my foot on the third step from the top. It creaked, the exact tired complaint I'd carried in my head for months.

And something broke open. I stood in the stairwell and wept, one hand on the cold painted rail, the smell of the building all around me, boiled cabbage and floor polish and somebody's coffee.

A door opened above me. "Papa?"

She was at the top of the stairs in her socks. A small figure in a green jumper, her hair in two uneven plaits, one slipping. She stood there as if she'd been shot, hands at her sides, eyes huge. Then she ran. She jumped from the bottom step, the way she always does, to make it creak on purpose, and hit me in the chest with her whole weight, and I dropped the bag, and then I was holding her, and she was holding me, and neither of us could breathe.

"You're scratchy," she said into my neck.

"I know."

"You smell weird."

"I know."

"Don't go."

I said nothing. I couldn't. I just held her. Over her shoulder, Katya stood in the doorway in her gray sweater, one hand pressed flat against the frame as though the flat might fall down. She didn't run. She waited. When I finally looked up, she took one step toward me and then another. And then she put her arms round the two of us and her forehead against my jaw.

"You're home," she said.

"Seven days."

"You're *home,*" she said again, fiercely. "Shut up."

I don't remember the rest of the first hour. A kitchen. A bath she ran for me, hotter than I'd allowed myself to want. I sat in it for forty minutes, in the quiet, watching the steam, hearing through the wall the small domestic sounds of two people moving about a flat, a kettle, a cupboard, Sonya saying something to the cat. It was the loudest silence of my life.

I couldn't sleep that night. The mattress was too soft. The room too quiet. No stove. No snoring. No dog. I lay awake until three, listening, and then, finally, got up and sat on the kitchen floor with my back to the fridge, and in the dark, a small hand slid into mine. Sonya, in her nightdress, silent. She sat down next to me, put her head on my arm, and we stayed there together until morning, not speaking.

# Day 66 — Notes: The Culture Shock of Quiet

I wasn't ready for this part.

**What I noticed in the first twenty-four hours:**
- **Doors.** I kept closing them. Then not wanting to.
- **Hot water.** On demand, forever. I wept. I'm not ashamed.
- **Sound.** The fridge. The traffic. A neighbor's TV.
- **Light.** Switches, bulbs, screens. So much of it.
- **Food.** An entire shelf. I stood in the shop for ten minutes.
- **People.** Everyone looks fine. It's alarming.
- **Time.** It runs in a straight line. I kept losing it.

**What I'm trying to remember:** This is why. This room. This soft, ordinary, enormous peace.

What small luxury at home would stop you in your tracks?
