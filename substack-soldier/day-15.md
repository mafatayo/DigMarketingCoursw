# Day 15 — Dispatch: Night Watch

Watch is two hours, sometimes three, on a crate at the edge of the position with a thermos and a pair of binoculars you're not supposed to need. I drew the middle watch tonight, midnight to two. The worst slot, because you're too awake to sleep and too tired to think straight.

I went up with a blanket over my shoulders and the Colonel padding behind me. The cold had settled into a hard clean silence, the kind you can hear. Stars. A lot of stars, more than I'd seen since I was a boy, because no one's turned on a light in this valley for months. Orion low in the south. The Plough tipped over like it'd been dropped.

The first hour was fine. I watched the line of the horizon, a faint charcoal against black, and I listened. I did my job. I rehearsed the checklist in my head, the way you do: left, center, right, sound, smell, movement. Left, center, right.

The second hour was different. The mind drifts. You can't stop it. I thought about my father, who died when I was twenty-six, and who once drove four hours to bring me a forgotten jacket. I thought about the apartment, the creak of the third stair, how Sonya jumps from the bottom step on purpose to make it creak. I thought about my last project before the call, a little documentary about a beekeeper in the Carpathians. I'd recorded his hives. The sound of forty thousand bees, a living low hum, like a choir that never ends.

I wondered if the bees were still there. I wondered if anyone was recording them. I wondered if it mattered, and decided that yes, it did, it mattered more than almost anything, someone holding up a microphone to a small stubborn life.

Then a rustle, somewhere to the left. My whole body snapped to attention. The Colonel's ears went up. We both stared into the dark.

A hedgehog, trundling along the wire. Absurdly slow. Absolutely serious about its business.

I breathed out. It took a long time.

At two, Bohdan came up, hair wild, rubbing his eyes. "Anything?"

"A hedgehog," I said.

"Dangerous?"

"Very."

He took my blanket, which wasn't a thing I'd offered, and I went down the steps into the warm. The room smelled of tea and old smoke and the slow steady breath of sleeping men. I lay in my bunk with the whole sky still in my head.

# Day 15 — Notes: The Thinking Hours

What I think about at 1 a.m. on watch. In order of frequency.

1. Home. Always. The house, the smell, the specific shape of Katya's shoulder.
2. Food. Specific, embarrassing food.
3. What I should have said to people. Old arguments. Mostly with my brother.
4. The future. Small things first. Where to put the couch.
5. Whether I'm doing this right.
6. The sound of bees.

**What helps:**
- A task. Count the stars in a patch, count your breaths.
- A small song, hummed.
- Reciting a poem, if you know one. Zenko recites, quietly, over the radio when the line is dead.
- Writing. I write it down in the dark in pencil, which looks like scribble in the morning but means something to me.

**What doesn't help:** Checking the time. It makes it longer.
