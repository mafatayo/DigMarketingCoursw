*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 81 — Dispatch: The Veteran's Question

A man came to visit from the rear, a veteran of an earlier phase of all this, with a prosthetic leg and a stick, and he asked us the question none of us could answer.

His name was Ihor. He was about forty-five, broad, balding, with the quiet, humorous bearing of someone who'd made peace with a great deal. He'd come to speak to the new men, he said, "just to chat." We sat on crates in a circle and he told us stories. Some funny. A few not. At one point he rapped his leg with his knuckle, and it gave a hollow knock. "This one's called Boris," he said. "Boris and I have an understanding."

Then, near the end, he looked round the circle and said, "Here's what no one asks until it's too late. When it's over, what will you *do?*"

Silence. Even the Colonel seemed to listen.

"I mean it," Ihor said. "Not what you'll feel. What you'll do on a Monday. At nine o'clock. In the morning. When you wake in your own bed and there's no one telling you where to go."

Nobody answered. I think we'd each assumed, quietly, that the question would never be ours to face. That the war would simply end, and life would resume, by some obscure magic, exactly where we'd left it.

"I was a welder," said Ihor, gently. "I assumed I'd weld. Then my hands did not like the heat. Then my back did not like the angle. Then I realized I had no idea who I was without the war, or without the welding. I'm good now. I teach apprentices. But it took two years of being miserable to find out."

"What would you tell us?" said Bohdan.

Ihor considered. "Start thinking now. Not planning. Thinking. What do you *like?* What's something you did as a boy that made time vanish? Where do you feel useful? Write it down. Keep the list in a pocket. You'll need it."

The conversation drifted afterward. But I saw the effect. All week, I noticed small signs. Bohdan, at his workbench, sketching a design for a small prosthetic hand. Zenko, drafting what might be the first page of a book. Vira asking Motor if he'd ever thought about running a bus depot, and Motor, for the first time in my hearing, laughing so hard he had to wipe his eyes. Halyna said she'd "like a very small clinic in a very small village with a very large garden."

I wrote my own list, the first line trembling a little. *Sound. Quiet. A room that's mine.* I don't know what it means yet. But it's mine, and I've put it in my pocket.

# Day 81 — Notes: After the War, What Job?

A list for anyone with a Monday coming.

1. **Don't wait for the end to start thinking.**
2. **List what you loved as a child.** The answer's often there.
3. **List what you're good at, now.** It's different than before.
4. **Talk to someone who's done it.** Veterans are generous.
5. **Take your time.** Two years is normal.
6. **Be gentle with the first job.** It needn't be the last.
7. **Remember: you're not one thing.** A soldier is not all you are.

What would you do, on a Monday, with no one telling you?
