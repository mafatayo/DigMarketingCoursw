# Day 60 — Dispatch: Day Sixty

Two months, and I want to take my own temperature.

It's evening, the lamp low, the dugout mostly asleep. Vira's on watch. Halyna is mending something by the stove, her glasses on the end of her nose. The Colonel is stretched across the foot of my bunk, snoring in short, wheezy, indignant bursts. I'm sitting up with the notebook on my knee, trying to take stock.

Physically: I'm thinner, harder, grayer. A new line has appeared at the corner of each eye. My hands are rough as bark. I can lift more than I could when I arrived, and sleep less, and walk farther in worse boots. I've stopped noticing the cold, mostly. That scares me a little.

Mentally: it's stranger. I've changed, but not in the direction I'd predicted. I expected to harden. I expected a sort of flinty, numb resolve. Instead I've gone soft in odd places. A song on the radio can undo me. A child's drawing, a stranger's pickle, a dog's weight against my leg. I cry more than I did at home. Not in grief, mostly. In something that feels like recognition.

I've also become, I think, a little more honest. There's no room for pretending here. If I'm sad, Halyna notices. If I'm lying, Vira catches me. If I'm being selfish, Motor hands me a bucket. It's a hard school, but a good one.

What have I lost? Certainty. Some sleep. Some innocence about what a person can do to another. A few friends, in ways I can't yet name. The ability to be bored by the news.

What have I gained? A family of strangers. A sense of how little I need. An understanding of how large an ordinary kindness can be. A habit of looking at the sky.

I think about the man at the bus stop on Day 1 with his heavy bag and his borrowed courage. I'd like to tell him: you were right to be frightened. You were also stronger than you knew.

Zenko woke while I was writing. He sat up, blinking, in his two scarves, and said, in the dark, "Are you composing?"

"A bit."

"Good. A man who writes is a man who intends to survive."

"Is that Plutarch?"

"It's me," said Zenko. "I'm fairly sure it's true." He lay down again. After a pause: "Sixty days, my friend. Well done."

I turned out the lamp. In the dark, the dugout breathed around me, seven sleepers and a dog and a stove ticking down. I listened. It sounded like a house.

# Day 60 — Notes: Reassessing

An honest checklist.

**What I'm proud of:**
- That I'm still writing.
- That I've learned the new guy's name, and his mother's.
- That I haven't stopped calling home.

**What I'm worried about:**
- That I'm getting used to it.
- That I'll forget how the quiet sounds.
- That I'll be a stranger to Sonya.

**What I want for the next thirty days:**
1. To stay kind.
2. To keep the notebook.
3. To learn the whole of Motor's song.
4. To eat well, sleep when I can, and write one honest letter.

Thank you for being here for the first two-thirds. The last part, I think, is the hardest. I'd rather not do it alone.
