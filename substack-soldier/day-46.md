*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 46 — Dispatch: The Lost Kit

Yaroslav lost his bag.

Not in any exciting way. We were moving a few supplies between positions, a short, muddy, entirely ordinary job, and somewhere between the truck and the dugout his backpack slipped from the tailgate and was gone. By the time he noticed, a quarter of an hour later, the track was empty behind us. No bag. Just a long brown ribbon of ruts.

He came in white-faced. "I lost it. I lost everything."

"Everything?" said Motor.

"My spare socks. My razor. My..." He swallowed. "My mother's letters."

Nobody said anything for a second. Then Halyna set down her mug, stood up, and said, "All right. Let's make a list."

She gets practical when she's moved. She took a pencil and a scrap of paper and sat Yaroslav on a crate. "What do you need? Not what you miss. What you need. We'll start there."

He listed. Socks. Underwear. A hat. A spare shirt. A toothbrush. Soap. A notebook. The list got longer, sadder, and then, one by one, the people in the dugout started to move.

Vira handed over a pair of thick gray socks. "They're clean," she said, "mostly." Bohdan went to his corner and came back with a razor. "It's not very good," he said. "It's what I had." Zenko, with great ceremony, took off one of his two scarves and draped it round Yaroslav's neck. "A gentleman never loses both," he said. Motor gave a hat, and a tin of tobacco he didn't smoke, and a short, rough lecture on tying down your gear.

I gave him a notebook and a pencil.

In less than half an hour, the small pile at his feet was nearly everything on the list. A toothbrush from someone. A mug. A comb, with three teeth missing. A shirt that had once been mine and was a little too long for him. He sat in the middle of the pile with his hands on his knees and an expression I can't describe, a mix of embarrassment and astonishment and something close to worship.

"I can't take all this."

"You can," said Halyna. "That's how it works."

"But I'll pay you back."

"With what?"

"With... with next month's..."

"Yaroslav," said Motor. "It's socks."

He wept then, a bit, wiping his face on the new scarf. And then he laughed, because Vira told him the scarf smelled of history, which made Zenko offended.

The letters, though. His mother's. Those couldn't be replaced. That night, Motor took a lantern and went back out along the track alone. I watched him go. Two hours later he returned, mud to his knees, silent, and set a crumpled backpack on the bunk. He didn't say where he'd found it. He just hummed his four bars.

# Day 46 — Notes: What You Can Replace

A short list. Maybe the shortest one I've written.

**Replaceable:**
- Socks. Gloves. Hats. Razors. Mugs. Tools.
- Even a rifle. Even a radio.
- A pair of boots, at a push.

**Not replaceable:**
- Letters.
- Photographs.
- A child's drawing.
- The pencil your father gave you.
- Anything with a voice in it.

**What I'd tell anyone leaving home:** Scan the pictures. Photograph the letters. Back it all up somewhere safe. And then carry the originals anyway, because the paper holds something the copy doesn't.

**What I understand now about generosity:** It's easiest when the thing is replaceable. It's real when it isn't.

Motor walked back into the dark for a bag of letters. I'm still thinking about that.
