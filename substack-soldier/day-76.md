*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 76 — Dispatch: Thaw

The mud came back, and it had brought friends.

If the first mud was a lesson, this was a doctorate. The thaw loosened the whole world at once. The frozen ruts turned to soup. The paths became canals. The trench floor, firm all winter, went to a thick brown porridge that took your boot and kept it. Yaroslav lost two in a single morning and stood in the track on one leg, as if posing for a statue of Defeat.

"Welcome to spring," said Vira, from the dugout steps, with a mug.

"Help."

"No."

"*Please.*"

"I'm saving my strength for the laughing."

It took three of us to extract him. By the time we were done, we were all wearing a thin brown coat to the knee and none of us could speak for laughter. Motor, who'd arrived to see what the noise was, looked at us for a long, solemn moment, said "Disgraceful," and then slowly sat down on a crate and began to shake with silent mirth.

It's astonishing what mud does to a person's dignity. It levels everyone. I've seen generals, visiting on inspection, with their splendid polished boots buried to the ankle, standing in the middle of a field with a face like a man who has just seen his own funeral. I've seen the sternest corporal slide gracefully on his back down a bank. There's an old, democratic joy in watching someone fall into mud, a joy that predates all armies.

But there's also grief in it. The thaw brings the old fields back to life, and with them everything the winter had hidden. I'll be careful here. Things appear, uncovered by the melting. Things from before. The ground gives up what it has kept. It's a reminder that this has been going on a long time, and that every field here is a ledger.

Halyna, I noticed, went quiet on the first day of the real melt. She stood at the edge of the dugout, looking out over the gray soaked field, arms folded. When I came up beside her, she said, softly, "It's very beautiful. And I hate it."

"The spring?"

"The way it brings everything back."

We stood there. A thin brown stream was making its way along the track, glinting. Far off, a lark was singing, so high you could hardly see it.

"Come in," I said. "Tea."

"Yes," she said. "Tea."

# Day 76 — Notes: Mud Season Returns

A manual for the second round.

1. **Gaiters.** Old trousers cut into leg wraps. Ugly. Brilliant.
2. **Plastic bags over socks.** Inside the boot. Don't ask.
3. **Planks.** Anything flat. A path of planks is a luxury.
4. **Walk like a heron.** Lift. Place. Don't slide.
5. **Clean the boots at every door.** You'll fail. Do it anyway.
6. **Laugh.** It's the only thing that doesn't stick.

**What the mud teaches:** Humility. Everyone falls. Help the person who does.
