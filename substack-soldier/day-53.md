# Day 53 — Dispatch: Rumors Reach Even Here

The rumor came on a Tuesday, and by Wednesday it had legs, a beard, and a pension.

It started as a whisper that the fighting in a distant region had ended. By noon, it was a ceasefire. By evening, an agreement. By night, someone in the next dugout swore he'd heard on the radio that we'd all be home by the weekend. A man in a neighboring position cried with joy. A second began packing.

We heard it the way you hear a bell far off: faint, distorted, pleasant. Bohdan ran in with his eyes enormous. "Is it true?"

"Who told you?"

"Taras told me. Taras heard from Oleh. Oleh said the driver said."

"The driver of what?"

"A truck."

"That," said Zenko, with the weary patience of a man who has seen forty thousand years of rumor, "is not a source. That is a *vehicle.*"

I watched the thing spread through the whole position like water through cloth. It's astonishing how a story grows. Every retelling adds a detail. By the fifth, the rumor had acquired a date, a signature, a treaty and, inexplicably, a banquet.

Motor held a meeting. He sat us down in a row and spoke in his flat, solid, unhurried voice. "I want to say something. Maybe it's true. Maybe not. In either case, nothing has changed for us. We have our jobs. We're here until someone with real authority tells us otherwise. Until then, we keep the stove lit and the watch kept. Understood?"

"Yes, Sergeant."

"And if anyone tells you something, ask three questions. Who said it? How do they know? What do they want?"

"What do they want?" said Yaroslav.

"Everyone wants something," said Motor.

The rumor died the next morning, quietly, the way they do. A supply officer came through and shrugged. "No ceasefire. Not that I know of." And a ripple of disappointment ran through the position, the soft collective sag of men who'd allowed themselves a day of hope.

But here's the odd thing. Nobody was angry. Not exactly. Bohdan said, "It was nice for a day, though," and Vira said, "Yes. It was." And I think that was all of it. A rumor is a kind of dream. It tells you what you want. And for a day, we'd gotten to want it out loud.

# Day 53 — Notes: Spotting Disinformation

Some rules we use. They aren't perfect. They help.

1. **If it makes you very angry, pause.** Anger is a lever.
2. **If it makes you very happy, pause.** So is hope.
3. **Who benefits from you believing it?**
4. **Can you find the original source?** Not a screenshot of a screenshot.
5. **Is there a photo?** Check it. Old photos wear new captions.
6. **Is it too clean?** Real events are messy.
7. **Wait a day.** Most rumors die by lunch.
8. **Tell someone you trust.** Don't just pass it along.

**The cost of a rumor:** Morale, trust, sometimes lives.

**The cost of a pause:** A few hours of not knowing.
