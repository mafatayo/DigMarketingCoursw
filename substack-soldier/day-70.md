*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 70 — Dispatch: Politics in the Trench

We don't talk about politics much, which people find surprising.

They imagine, I think, long fiery debates in the lamplight, fists on tables, theories and denunciations. There are some. But more often, politics here arrives sideways, as a sigh, or a joke, or a very long silence after the news.

Last night it arrived as Bohdan. He'd been listening to something on his phone, a clip of someone in a good suit making a promise, and he took out one earbud and said, flatly, "I wonder how many of them have ever carried water."

Nobody answered. Motor's mustache twitched.

"It's not fair," Vira said at last. "They have a different job."

"I know."

"It's a hard job."

"I know it is." Bohdan scratched his neck. "I just wish... I wish they knew what it was like. Even once. For a day."

Zenko, a thoughtful look on his face, closed his book. "May I offer a small observation? I taught civics, for a year, before history. The one thing I'm sure of is that every government has been out of touch with its soldiers. Always. Rome. Byzantium. The Cossack Hetmans. It is practically a law of nature."

"That doesn't make it okay," said Bohdan.

"No. It makes it permanent. The question isn't whether we're forgotten by the people above. The question is what we build below."

Halyna, dry: "Soup, mostly."

"Soup is political," said Zenko. "Soup is the most political thing there is."

I won't tell you what each of us thinks. It varies. We come from different towns, different families, different loyalties. One of us voted one way, one another, one not at all. Motor says he doesn't vote, he only fixes things. But here's what I've noticed: when the shells fall, nobody asks who you voted for. When a man's bleeding, no one checks his manifesto.

There's a kind of politics that's only about the person next to you. Do they have water? Are they warm? Did they sleep? Have they been heard? It's smaller and more honest than the other kind. If the whole country could do it for one week, I think we'd be a different place.

At midnight I stepped outside, as I often do. Bohdan was standing by the post, his phone dark in his pocket. We stood there for a bit without saying anything. Finally he said, "I don't want to be bitter."

"Then don't."

"How?"

"I don't know yet," I said. "Ask me in a year."

# Day 70 — Notes: Trust and Institutions

I'm not an expert. I'm a man in a hole who thinks about it.

**What I trust:**
- The person beside me.
- The driver who brings the water.
- The nurse who checks my feet.
- A well-run supply chain, wherever it exists.

**What I want from those above:**
1. Honesty. Even when it's bad.
2. Competence. Plain, boring, reliable.
3. Presence. To see where we live.
4. Fairness. In rotations, in leave, in praise.
5. Memory. Not to forget us when it's over.

**What I give in return:** Work. Patience. Loyalty, mostly to my friends.

I'm trying not to be cynical. It's harder than the digging.

What do you want from the people who lead you?
