*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 7 — Dispatch: The Sunday Silence

Sundays are different, and nobody has to say so.

There's no schedule that makes it so. The war doesn't take Sundays off, and I want to be clear about that, because people at home sometimes imagine a pause. There isn't one. But something in the dugout shifts anyway. Motor doesn't hand out chores until after breakfast. Vira turns the radio down to a murmur. Bohdan sleeps until nine, which would be a scandal on a Tuesday.

This morning Zenko shaved. Carefully, with cold water and a small mirror propped on a nail, while the rest of us watched like it was theater. He's the only one who does it every Sunday. "A man should have one day a week where he looks like himself," he said, drawing the blade down his cheek.

"Who are you looking like?" Vira said.

"A history teacher, madam. A great and noble profession."

"You look like my uncle," she said. "Before he went to prison."

Zenko considered this, then went on shaving. I liked that he didn't fight it.

Around ten I went up the steps and sat on a sandbag. The air was cold and clean, the kind that makes your teeth ache a little. There was frost on the wire. A bird, small and brown, ordinary, sat on a post about three meters from me and looked at me with total contempt. I looked back. We stayed like that for a minute, two professionals.

It's quiet on Sundays, but not empty. You hear everything. The wind in a plastic bag caught on a branch. A dripping somewhere. A generator muttering, far off. Someone coughing in another dugout, a flat dry cough that carries across the field like a message. I thought about how many people were hearing the same things right now, on both sides, standing in the same cold, thinking about their own kitchens.

That thought didn't make me feel peaceful. It made me feel strange, the way you feel when you realize the stranger on the train has a whole life behind his face.

Then it passed, because the bird flew off and Halyna called up the steps that there was a hot breakfast, actual eggs, someone had traded for eggs.

I'm told it's called church, in some units. A small service. A priest comes sometimes. Ours doesn't, at least not yet. We have eggs, and a man shaving, and a girl with a laugh like a slammed door telling her uncle's prison story to anyone who'll listen. It's enough.

I ate three eggs and felt, for about an hour, like a person.

# Day 7 — Notes: Week One Ledger

Seven days. Here's the honest accounting.

**Weight lost:** Probably three kilos. My belt has a new notch.
**Hours of sleep, average:** About five. Interrupted.
**Hot showers:** Zero. (Shower day is coming. I've been told to wait.)
**Times I've been scared:** I stopped counting at eleven.
**Times I've been bored:** More than that, which surprised me.
**Number of times I've laughed:** Lots. More than I'd expect. Bohdan is funnier than he knows.
**Letters received:** 1. Letters written: 4.
**Blisters:** Six. Halyna has opinions.
**Things I thought I'd miss, and don't:** Phone news. My own opinions about the news.
**Things I miss and didn't expect to:** Doors. Closing a door behind me. A door with a lock on it.
**Things I've learned:** How to tell the sound of an engine from the sound of a wind. How to light a stove in the dark. How to fall asleep with my boots on.

**The thing I didn't write down but should:** Nobody here has asked me why I came. They know. It wasn't a choice anyone made out of a fine speech. It was a phone call at 5 a.m.

If you'd keep a ledger of your own week, what would be in it?
