# Day 68 — Dispatch: Leave Ends

The seventh morning arrived the way a tooth arrives at the dentist.

I woke before the alarm, in the soft gray light, with Katya's arm across my chest and the weight of Sonya's feet on my shins, the two of them having migrated into our bed some time in the night. For a long moment I didn't move. I lay there and memorized it. The slow rise and fall of the blanket. The tick of the radiator. The faint smell of Katya's hair. The tiny whistle in my daughter's breath.

I've heard men describe it. "Fix it in your mind." I did. I tried to press every detail into the soft wax of memory, knowing it wouldn't hold, knowing that in a week it'd blur.

Breakfast was quiet. Katya made eggs, as I'd always wanted. She put them in front of me with a little too much force and said, "Eat." Sonya sat on my lap and fed me bread in small pieces, with total seriousness, like an apprentice nurse.

I'd packed the night before. A clean shirt. Chocolate. A jar of Katya's plum jam. Three of Sonya's drawings. A photograph, printed, of the three of us on the sofa, taken by a neighbor. And a small paper bag of poppy-seed rolls from the baker, who'd refused all payment.

At the door I made a mistake. I'd promised myself I wouldn't. But Sonya, serious, standing in the hall in her pajamas with Marmalade under one arm, said, "Papa, you're coming back."

"Yes."

"Promise."

I didn't answer for a moment. I knelt, put my hands on her shoulders. "I promise to try with everything I have," I said.

"That's not a promise."

"No," I said. "It's the truest thing I have."

She looked at me for a long time. Then, gravely, she put Marmalade in my hands. "He can go. But he has to come back, too."

I took the rabbit.

Katya didn't cry. She held my face between her palms, hard, the way you'd check a melon, and said, "You're thinner, and you're braver than you think. Be careful. Eat. Call." Then she kissed me. Then, lower, "Come home."

I walked down the stairs. On the third step from the top, it creaked. I nearly turned back.

I didn't. At the bottom I looked up. Two faces, small and pale, in the window of the fourth floor. A hand waved. A smaller hand, holding nothing.

I carried the rabbit in my breast pocket all the way to the junction. When Motor saw it, he said nothing at all, but he took my bag, and in the dugout that night, I saw him set a mug at my place.

# Day 68 — Notes: Going Back

The hardest thing I've done. Truly.

**What's hard:**
- The leaving. Obviously.
- The first night back. The silence, in reverse.
- Re-learning the cold.
- Being around people who've kept going without me, and seeing that they can.

**What helps:**
1. A task, immediately.
2. Familiar faces. Thank God for Motor.
3. A small object from home. I have a rabbit.
4. Telling someone. I told Halyna. She hugged me for a long time.
5. Writing it down.

**What I'm carrying now:** Six poppy-seed rolls. One toy rabbit. A promise I can't quite make. And a stronger reason than I had before.

I'm back.
