# Day 54 — Dispatch: A Friend Transfers Out

Taras, the guitar man, is leaving.

He came to tell us at dusk, wearing his big coat, with his long hands in his pockets. His unit's being moved. A different sector, a different job, a day's travel away. Orders came down that morning. He's got until tomorrow.

He stood at the top of the steps for a bit, as if reluctant to come down, and then he did, ducking under the blanket door. "I wanted to say goodbye properly," he said, "and I realized I don't know how."

Nobody did. That was the strange thing. For all the funerals and farewells in this war, there's no etiquette for the simple unremarkable leaving of a friend. No ceremony. No flag. He's just going. Next week he'll be somewhere else, and so will we, and we'll write, maybe. Or we won't.

"Sit," said Halyna. "Tea."

He sat. We gave him tea. Zenko gave him a short speech, which didn't need to be long. "You have been a gentleman and a musician. The first is rare. The second is rarer. May your strings always be more than three."

Taras smiled. "I'm leaving the guitar."

"You can't."

"It likes it here."

"It's your guitar."

"It's *ours,*" he said. "Somebody has to play it."

He looked around at us then, one by one, as if memorizing. I've watched men do it. A slow, deliberate sweep, the eyes touching each face. It's not sentimental. It's a kind of insurance.

When it came to me, he held my eyes a moment. "Look after the dog," he said.

"I will."

"He's your dog."

"He's the Colonel's."

"He's yours," said Taras.

He didn't hug anyone, he's not the type. He shook hands, long and firm, in the old way. With Motor, he did something I'd never seen: they pressed foreheads together briefly, two big tired men, like bulls. Then he left.

I went out to see him off. It was nearly dark. He walked toward the track with his bag over his shoulder, a long thin shape against the gray. He didn't turn. I watched until I couldn't tell him from the fence posts.

Back in the dugout, someone had put the guitar on his chair. Nobody touched it. We sat in a long, comfortable quiet, and after a while Bohdan picked it up and began, haltingly, to play the lullaby. Badly. With a few notes wrong. Nobody corrected him.

# Day 54 — Notes: Goodbyes Without Ceremony

What I've noticed about leaving.

- **Most people leave without drama.** A truck, a bag, a nod.
- **Some of us never say it.** Easier not to.
- **Some say it badly.** That's fine.
- **"See you" is the standard.** It's a promise and a hope.
- **You keep something they leave.** A guitar. A mug. A habit.
- **You don't always get the chance to say it.** Say it early.

**A small practice I've started:** When I like someone, I tell them. Out loud, plainly. "I'm glad you're here." It costs nothing. It might be the last thing they hear from me.

Who in your life deserves a plain "I'm glad you're here" this week?
