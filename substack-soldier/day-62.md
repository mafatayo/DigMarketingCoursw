# Day 62 — Dispatch: The Holiday Table

Twelve dishes is the tradition. That's the rule for the Holy Supper, and my grandmother would rise from the grave if she knew we'd managed three.

Still, we tried.

The evening began with a debate that ran, I think, four days. Who'd cook what. Where we'd find the ingredients. Whether the potatoes counted as one dish or two. Zenko, quoting some obscure source, claimed that the number twelve was symbolic and could be met with "creative interpretation." Halyna said, "You can't interpret borshch." Vira said, "Then bring the borshch."

In the end we assembled what we had. A pot of beet soup, made by Halyna from a tin and a miracle. A bowl of boiled potatoes with garlic. Pickled cucumbers from Babusya's last jar. Bread, still warm, courtesy of a village baker. A little dish of stewed dried fruit, called *uzvar,* made from a bag of apples and plums that a driver had left. A dish of beans. Honey, a spoon each. A bowl of kutia, the wheat porridge with poppy seed, brought by the chaplain, who'd walked three kilometers carrying it in a pot wrapped in a towel.

That made seven. Nobody said it wasn't twelve.

We cleared the crate-table and spread Zenko's embroidered cloth. Halyna set a candle in the middle, a stub in a mug. At the head of the table, by tradition, stood an empty chair, for whoever couldn't be there. We left it. Nobody mentioned why. Beside it we set a plate, with a spoonful of each, and a mug of tea.

The chaplain said the blessing, short and quiet. The snow ticked against the door. Then, one by one, we sat.

For a long moment, nobody ate. We looked at the table, at the candle, at each other in the soft yellow light, at the empty chair. I felt the shape of the whole year in that small space: everyone who was missing, everyone who was here.

Motor spoke first, gruffly. "I'd like to say something. I'm not good at it." He cleared his throat. "My wife is in Chernihiv, with a table like this. I'd like to think she's setting a plate for me. I'd like to think she knows I'm setting one for her."

Nobody said a word. Then Vira lifted her mug. "To the plates," she said.

"To the plates," we echoed.

We ate slowly, and everything tasted of someone's kitchen. Afterwards, Taras's guitar came out, and Yaroslav played the lullaby, better than before. And when the candle burned low, the Colonel lay down under the empty chair with a long sigh, as if he'd been appointed to guard it.

# Day 62 — Notes: Traditions, Adapted

How we kept the old things, in smaller shapes.

- **Twelve dishes → seven.** We counted the honey.
- **Hay under the tablecloth.** Yaroslav found a handful in a barn. We put it under the cloth.
- **Garlic at the doors.** Vira's. She's not superstitious. She also does it.
- **The first star.** We watched for it, and waited to eat.
- **The empty chair.** Always.
- **Carols.** Quietly, indoors.
- **A call home.** Everyone, one by one, in the cold.

**What I understand:** A tradition is a vessel. It doesn't matter if it's the right size. It only matters that you pour something in.
