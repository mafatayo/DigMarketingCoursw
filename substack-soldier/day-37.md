# Day 37 — Dispatch: Courage Is Logistics

Here's a thing I would not have believed on Day 1: the bravest person in our dugout may be a man named Oleh, who drives a truck full of cabbage.

He turned up this week, a stout, ruddy man with a thick neck and a belly that strained his jacket, who introduced himself with a handshake that could have cracked a walnut. He's a supply driver, one of a team who bring us everything. Water, fuel, food, batteries, boots, gloves, medicine, a letter from Katya, six crates of canned peaches that I suspect were stolen from a wedding.

He told us how it works, over tea, in the slow pleased voice of a man who's explained this to many people.

"You think of the war as soldiers, rifles, things that explode. But think about a single loaf of bread. Someone must bake it. Someone must drive it to a depot. Someone must load it on a truck. Someone must drive that truck down a road that isn't a road, in the dark, with the lights off. Someone must carry it down a trench. Someone must slice it. Someone must hand it to you. And you chew it. That's seven people for a loaf."

"That's a lot of people," said Bohdan.

"That's a *little* people. Wait until you count the fuel."

I've been thinking about this since. We tend to imagine heroism as a single dramatic act. A flag planted. A charge. But Oleh's heroism is dull and endless. It's getting up at three in the morning. Checking tires. Studying a map for the road least likely to have trouble. Driving for eleven hours, with the knowledge that the road can change between one trip and the next. Arriving. Unloading. Smiling. Leaving.

And he does it again the next day.

I asked him if he was ever afraid. He laughed, a big round laugh. "All the time. Do you think I like it? I'd rather be home in my garden with my tomatoes. But if I don't bring the bread, you don't eat. If you don't eat, you don't fight. It's very simple."

"That's brave," I said.

He looked embarrassed, genuinely, the way a big man does. "No, no. It's just work."

"It's both."

"Let me tell you what's brave," said Oleh. "Brave is a cook who makes a hot meal for sixty with two pots." He winked. "I'm only a driver."

He left at dawn. I stood at the top of the steps and watched his truck, battered, loaded to the roof with someone else's hope, rumble down the track and out of sight. The dust hung gold in the early light.

I will think of that truck whenever I hear someone say war is about guns.

# Day 37 — Notes: Supply Lines of Everything

An incomplete list of what has to arrive, safely, for us to exist.

**Daily:**
- Water
- Food
- Gas, for the stove
- Fuel, for the generator
- Batteries
- Mail

**Weekly:**
- Medicine
- Spare parts
- Boots, gloves, dry socks
- Tools
- New faces

**Constantly:**
- Information
- Morale
- Someone to say "thank you"

**What I understand now:** A war isn't only a fight. It's a very large, very fragile, very human delivery system. Every mile of that road is somebody's courage.

If there's a person in your life who "just does the logistics," thank them. Today. Out loud.
