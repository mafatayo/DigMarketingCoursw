*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 77 — Dispatch: First Birds

I've been watching a pair of larks, and I'm in love with them.

They arrived in the second week of the thaw, a small brown couple, unremarkable at a distance, and set up house in the tussocky grass just beyond the wire. Every morning, at an hour I'd thought was mine alone, the male rises straight up into the sky. A little column of song. He climbs and climbs, fluttering, pouring out a glittering unbroken stream of notes, until he's a speck, until he's gone, and the music still hangs there, coming from nowhere.

I stand on the sandbags with my tea and listen. I can't help it. In my old life I'd have wanted to record it. I'd have set up a shotgun mic in the grass and taped it for hours. Now I just listen with my ears and my skin and the whole of my slow, tired body.

I've learned the names. The larks, the wagtails, the little gray warblers in the bushes. A pair of storks has settled on a broken chimney in the village, an enormous untidy nest balanced on the crumbling brick. Zenko says they've been coming back to the same house for thirty years. "Constancy," he says, looking at them through his cracked binoculars. "Imagine. The bombs come and go, and the storks return."

"Storks are very reliable," says Motor.

"They are the most reliable creatures in Europe."

There's a thing about birds that I'd never thought about. They don't care. I mean it with love. They don't read the news. They don't know about fronts, lines, orders. A lark sings because it's spring, and its mate is near, and the sun is up. Nothing in its small brain is about us. It's the most restful thing in the world.

Last Tuesday, I found a nest in a crushed helmet. A rusted helmet, discarded, half sunk in the grass. A small round cup of dry stems and feathers had been tucked inside it, and in the cup lay four tiny speckled eggs. I looked at them for a long time, kneeling in the wet grass. It was almost too much.

I told no one, at first. Then I told Halyna. We've agreed to leave it. We walk wide round it. We carry the news like a secret.

This morning, one of the eggs had hatched. Just a wet, blind, shivering scrap, with a huge beak and a mouth like a small red door. I'm not ashamed to say I cried.

# Day 77 — Notes: Nature Keeps Going

What I'm learning from the field.

- **Grass grows through everything.** Concrete. Rust. Old wire.
- **Birds nest in the odd places.** A helmet. A pipe. An engine.
- **Flowers come up in the churned earth.** Poppies, mostly. Red as an argument.
- **The fox I named myself after is still here.** I saw him.
- **Insects return first.** A good sign, scientifically. A bad sign, for Vira.
- **The land forgives, slowly.** It doesn't forget, but it forgives.

**What it gives me:** A strange, steady faith. Not in a result. In life.

What small living thing have you been watching lately?
