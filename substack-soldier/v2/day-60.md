# Day 60 — Dispatch: Pockets

**Bottom line:** At sixty days I emptied my pockets, and I still wish I'd gone to Bohdan.

Sixty days, and I had nothing to say about it, so I emptied my pockets onto the bunk instead. Halyna says it's a way to take stock when the head won't.

Left breast pocket: a rabbit. No, not yet, that's later. Left breast pocket: Sonya's drawing of me with the enormous hands, folded in four, soft as cloth now. Katya's last letter, gone gray at the creases. A pencil stub. A photograph of the three of us on the sofa, curling.

Right breast: a tourniquet, rolled, with a strip of tape on it and the time written on my own arm in marker that I never washed off. 14:07. Faded but still there.

Trouser pocket, left: a red headlamp, the strap broken. A packet of foot powder, almost empty. A button I don't recognize. Three sunflower seeds. A stranger's blue paper heart, folded very small, from the girl abroad.

Trouser pocket, right: a lighter I don't use. A folded page from the notebook, *Names*, with Bohdan's at the top and under it everyone else's.

That's it. That's what I own.

I put it all back. It took a while to remember which went where, and then I realized I'd put the page with the names in my left pocket, over the heart, and that I'd done it without deciding, and I moved it back to the right and then moved it again.

Zenko woke up, in the other bunk, and looked at me over his glasses. "Inventory?"

"A bit."

"Good. A man who counts his pockets intends to need them." He rolled over. "Sixty days. Well done."

"Is that Plutarch?"

"It's me. I'm fairly sure it's true."

The Colonel was asleep across my feet. Halyna was awake, mending a sock with her glasses on the end of her nose; she raised her eyebrows at me once and went back to the sock. Vira was on watch. I could hear Motor breathing in the dark like a bellows.

If I'd had to write an honest summary it would say that I'm thinner and grayer and I cry more than I did at home, mostly not for grief. That I've lost some sleep and some certainty and two friends, depending how you count. That I wish I'd gone to him. I stood by the sandbags and I wish I'd gone.

I turned out the lamp. I could hear the ground, a long way off, doing whatever it does. The dugout sounded like a house.

# Day 60 — Notes: What I'd Ask

**Bottom line:** Three requests: keep looking, ask the ordinary, and remember both sides have mothers.

I'd ask three things.

That you keep looking. That you ask for the ordinary details, the bread, the dog, the window. And that you tell someone, in the room where you work, that the people on both sides of this line have mothers.

That's it. That's the whole appeal.

*Dispatches from the Line is a work of fiction.*
