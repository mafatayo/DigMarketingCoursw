*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 60 — Dispatch: Sixty

Two months, and I want to take my own temperature.

It's evening. The lamp low, the dugout mostly asleep. Vira's on watch. Halyna is mending something by the stove with her glasses on the end of her nose. The Colonel stretched across the foot of my bunk, snoring in short indignant wheezes. I'm sitting up with the notebook on my knee.

Physically: thinner, harder, grayer. A new line at the corner of each eye. Hands rough as bark. I can lift more than when I arrived, and sleep less, and walk farther in worse boots. I've stopped noticing the cold. That scares me a little.

Mentally: stranger. I expected to harden. I expected a flinty numb resolve. Instead I've gone soft in odd places. A song on the radio can undo me. A stranger's pickle. A dog's weight against my leg. I cry more than I did at home. Not for grief, mostly. For something that feels like recognition.

I've also become more honest. There's no room for pretending. If I'm sad, Halyna notices. If I'm lying, Vira catches me. If I'm being selfish, Motor hands me a bucket. A hard school, but a good one.

I want to say what I've lost, and I'll try to be exact. Certainty. Some sleep. Some innocence about what a person can do to another. A friend, two friends, depending on how you count. The ability to be bored by the news.

What I've gained: a family of strangers, a sense of how little I need, an understanding of how large an ordinary kindness can be.

And a debt. I can't put it any other way. I'm now carrying the name of a boy who flew drones and said sorry, and a razor, and a swallow, and a notebook, and I don't know where to put them down. I've realized I can't. That's the thing. They go where I go.

I think about the man at the bus stop on Day 1 with his heavy bag and borrowed courage. I'd like to tell him: you were right to be frightened. You were also stronger than you knew. And don't stand by the sandbags next time. Go to him.

Zenko woke while I was writing. He sat up blinking in two scarves and said, in the dark, "Composing?"

"A bit."

"Good. A man who writes is a man who intends to survive."

"Plutarch?"

"Me. I'm fairly sure it's true." He lay down. After a pause: "Sixty days. Well done."

I turned out the lamp. The dugout breathed round me, six sleepers and a dog and a stove ticking down. I listened. It sounded like a house.

# Day 60 — Notes: Account

Proud of: still writing. Learning the new kid's name, and his mother's. Still calling home.

Worried about: getting used to it. Forgetting how quiet sounds. Being a stranger to Sonya.

For the next thirty days: stay kind. Keep the book. Learn the whole of Motor's song. Eat, sleep when I can, write one honest letter.
