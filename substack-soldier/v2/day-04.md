# Day 4 — Dispatch: 11:40

We missed the 11:40 tea.

There's a schedule. Vira keeps it on a scrap of cardboard by the door, in pencil, rubbed out and rewritten every day. *Tea 6:10. Tea 11:40. Tea, if possible, 4.* There's a fourth column headed "if they're bored." The point is that you can't light the burner whenever you like. Smoke is a signal, a thin gray thread out of a hole in the ground, and the other side has things in the air that sit up there for hours looking for exactly that. So the stove is lit in the gaps between flights, and everyone knows the gaps by heart, the way people at home know their bus times.

At 11:36 there was a buzzing. Thin, patient, somewhere over the tree line. Nobody said "drone." Nobody said anything. We sat very still round a cold kettle, six adults, like a séance. Bohdan went white and said "That's not ours," and nobody answered. Zenko, to be doing something, read the label on the tea tin. Twice.

It passed after forty minutes. Halyna lit the burner in about four seconds. The first mug went to Bohdan.

I'd been on the new rule for four days by then and I'll explain it, because it's the only one I've seen people obey without being told: whoever empties the kettle fills it. That's it. I watched a man who'd been awake close to two days walk to the water canister before he lay down because he'd poured the last cup. The kettle stood full on the burner for whoever came next. Nobody thanked him. Nobody ever does. It would be like thanking someone for breathing out.

Before I'd learned that, Zenko watched me put the used tea bag back in the jar, and made a small mark in his notebook. He keeps it in a plastic bag against the damp. I've never asked what the mark was.

The day before the séance I'd been outside digging six hours, in a wet cold that gets in behind your ears, and when I came down the steps nobody said hello. Halyna put a mug in my hands and closed my fingers round it, one at a time, like I was five. I almost cried. I want that noted as *almost.* I considered it for a good minute, which in this company counts.

What I haven't worked out is who the column "if they're bored" is for.

# Day 4 — Notes: Rankings

Buckwheat with tinned meat: 8. Twelve minutes, never lets you down.
Instant noodles: 6. Bohdan eats them dry, as a snack, which I find upsetting.
Cold porridge from a bag: 2. We don't discuss it.
Borshch, when a volunteer brings it: off the scale.
Anything a grandmother sent: infinity.
