# Day 31 — Dispatch: The Bird

**Bottom line:** An enemy drone watched us for eleven minutes; an hour later one hit the next position.

You hear it before you decide to.

I was outside the dugout, mid-yawn, a mug in my hand, when the sound slid into the morning. High and thin, a mosquito in a dark bedroom, except the mosquito had a purpose. Everybody outside stopped. Nobody dove for cover. We just stopped, the way a room does when someone says your name.

I didn't look up. You learn not to. Looking up is what it wants, Motor says, a face, a shape, a man who stands still. "Don't point, don't stare, don't run in the open. Walk like you've got nothing interesting to hide, and get under something."

So I walked. Slowly, carrying a mug of tea, toward the door, and my skin felt as if someone were holding a cold hand an inch above it. The buzz hung there, drifting, patient. Somewhere above the gray sky a small machine with a camera was looking at the roof of my life.

Inside, Vira was already on the net, voice flat and quick. Bohdan came in behind me looking ill. "That's not ours."

"I know."

"That's *theirs.*"

"I know, Bohdan."

He sat down heavily. He flies little machines all day. He knows how they handle, how they sing, how they tire in the cold. He told me once that after a long flight he feels the drone is an extension of his own eyes. And now someone else's was over us and he knew exactly how it was looking.

"They can see the stove smoke," he said. "They can see the path."

"They see lots of paths."

"Yes." He rubbed his face. "It's strange. I'm not scared of guns. Guns are loud and then they stop. This is just looking."

It went away after eleven minutes, as if bored. We waited another half hour anyway. Nobody spoke. Halyna put the kettle on very softly, as if the sound of the lid might call it back.

I've been trying to understand why the drone is worse than anything else. I think it's that it doesn't hate you. It's a patient eye, a small cold intelligence, and the thing to fear is the person at the other end, at a screen, with a joystick and a cup of coffee, deciding.

An hour later one of them did decide, though not about us: about the next position, not far off. We heard it, a short flat bang, not very big. I stood in the doorway and counted the seconds until the net came alive. Then a voice, calm, asking for a medic. Halyna was gone before it finished.

She came back at dusk and went straight to the bucket. When Bohdan asked, she said, "He'll keep the foot." That was the only thing she said all evening.

Later Bohdan flew a patrol of his own. I stood behind him and watched the little screen. A road, a line of trees, a bright flat sky. It's the same machine, the same thing. We're no different. This is what the century has done to all of us.

**Tactical read.** Aerial surveillance often precedes fires. When a drone loiters, the chance of a strike on that or a neighboring position rises, so crews take cover and report.

**OPSEC.** Positions, orientation of the line and distances are altered.

# Day 31 — Notes: Not Looking for Us

**Bottom line:** Not everything overhead is looking for soldiers.

Some of the machines overhead aren't looking for soldiers at all. I can't say more than that. A bakery has a roof and so does a bus. They don't need to be what they were looking for.

It's why the grandmother in the village now hangs her washing inside.
