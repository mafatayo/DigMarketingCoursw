# Day 38 — Dispatch: The Lieutenant

**Bottom line:** A very young lieutenant arrived, admitted he knew nothing, and learned to carry water the right way.

We got a new lieutenant. He looks fifteen.

Maksym is twenty-four, he assures us, with a boy's smooth face, big dark eyes, and a haircut so regulation it hurts. He arrived on a Tuesday with a spotless map case and a rucksack clearly packed by a mother, stood in the doorway, saluted, and said in a voice that cracked on the second word, "Sergeant. Lieutenant Maksym Koval. I'm... pleased to be here."

Motor looked him over from boots to cap. "Sit."

I'd expected resentment. When a green officer arrives the old hands circle like cats. But what I saw was something quieter. Halyna put a mug in his hands. Vira told him where to put his boots. Zenko explained the layout of the dugout as if conducting a tour of a cathedral.

Maksym drank his tea. Very clean hands, a tiny nick on one thumb from shaving. He looked round the dugout the way a child looks at a world he's been told about.

"I want to say something," he said at last, and everyone stopped. "I've read the manuals. I did the courses. I know how to give orders. But I've never actually been here. I'd like you to know that I know that."

Silence. Then Motor, grudgingly: "That's the first sensible thing an officer's said to me this year."

"I'll need you to teach me. If you're willing."

"We're not teachers."

"Zenko is."

"Zenko is *definitely* a teacher," said Zenko, delighted.

I saw the shape of it. The old problem. Rank without experience, experience without rank. It could be a disaster or a family.

He asked questions, plenty, small ones. "What does that sound mean?" "How do you light that stove?" "Why does the tea go first?" He took notes. When Motor corrected him he said "Understood, thank you," and did it again, right.

On the fifth day he did something I'd never seen an officer do. He carried water. All the way from the canisters, two buckets, in the rain, unasked, staggering. Motor watched from the steps.

"Lieutenant. You've got the buckets wrong. Carry one at a time. Your back lasts longer."

"Thank you, Sergeant."

"Next time."

Maksym grinned enormously. I think it was the proudest moment of his life.

And the next morning he got orders, which he read with his face going carefully blank. A change of plans from above. The section was to hold its position "regardless of developments on the flanks." He read it aloud in the voice of someone repeating something he's been told to repeat. Then he said, to us, to the stove, to nobody, "I don't think they know what it's like here."

"No," said Motor.

"I'm going to tell them."

"Good," said Motor. "They won't listen. But say it."

# Day 38 — Notes: Nineteen

**Bottom line:** The soldiers are getting younger.

They're getting younger. I won't say that as a complaint, only a fact. Yaroslav is nineteen and arrived with boots too clean for the mud.

Somewhere a mother signed a form. I'd like her to know that someone shared his soup with him on the first night.
