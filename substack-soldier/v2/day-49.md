# Day 49 — Dispatch: The Chaplain

He came the morning after, as if somebody had sent for him, though no one had.

A small man, perhaps sixty, round gray beard, a heavy brown coat that had once been black. A cross at his neck on a thin cord, wrapped in a bit of cloth against the cold. He walked down the dugout steps carefully with one hand on the wall, paused at the bottom for his eyes to adjust, and said, "Peace to this house."

Nobody knew how to answer. Motor said, finally, "Father."

"Not Father," the man said, smiling. "Mykhailo. Today I'm only a visitor. I heard there was a hard day here."

He sat on a crate. He declined tea, then accepted it. For the first twenty minutes he said nothing about God. He told us about the road he'd come by, a ruined bridge, a fox, a ridiculous mule called Vasyl who'd carried him half a mile. He had a quiet unhurried voice, like Zenko's but slower, as if each sentence were being laid down like a stone.

At last, when the room had loosened, he said, "May I ask? Is there anyone who'd like to say a name?"

A long silence. The stove ticked. Then Vira, almost inaudibly: "Yes."

She said it. Then nothing else. The chaplain nodded slowly. "Thank you." Then around the table one by one we said it again. Halyna. Zenko. Yaroslav, whose voice cracked. Motor, a rough whisper. Me, last, with my mouth dry.

I'm not religious. I was baptized and then forgotten by the church, and I've mostly forgotten it back. But I'll tell you what that small ritual did. For the first time since the day before, the name had air to breathe. It was no longer a stone in the chest. It was a word in a room, said and heard.

He prayed briefly in the old language, one hand lifted. I didn't follow all of it. I closed my eyes and let the sounds wash over me, low and ancient, like water over stones. Beside me Yaroslav was crying. Motor had his cap against his chest.

Afterward, he asked if there was anything of Bohdan's that someone might carry. Halyna brought the swallow and the candle stub, from his pocket, in a small cloth. The chaplain held them for a while, and then gave them back to Vira, not to anyone else, and said, "Keep these until you know."

"Know what?"

"Who they're for."

When he left he put a hand on each of our shoulders, one by one, and said something short. Nobody has told me what he said to anyone else. To me, it was a few words. They went somewhere I haven't been since.

"Come back," said Motor at the door.

"I will," said Mykhailo. "Mind the mule."

# Day 49 — Notes: Faith

A girl in the next position keeps a tiny icon in her helmet. A cheerful atheist says grace over buckwheat "just in case." A Muslim volunteer prayed beside the chaplain today, shoulder to shoulder. Someone said, "I don't know what I believe, but I believe in this tea."

It gives grief somewhere to go. It gives words to the unsayable. Say the name. Out loud, in a room.
