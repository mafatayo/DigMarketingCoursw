*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 87 — Dispatch: What's Left

I've been trying to put together a short statement of what's left, after eighty-seven days. It's no manifesto, more the list at the bottom of a rucksack, the stuff still there when everything else has been unpacked.

I believe in small decency. I used to think it was a pleasant extra. I now think it's the structure the world hangs on. A cup of tea put in the right hand at the right moment holds up more than most treaties.

I believe fear and courage live in the same house. They share a bed. A man is brave when he's afraid and does the next thing anyway.

I believe people are better than the news makes them look, and worse than they believe themselves on bad days. Both. The trick is to hold both.

I believe in jokes. A good joke at the right moment is an act of love.

I believe in listening. It's the thing I'm best at and the thing the world needs most. The kind where you put your own thoughts down and let someone else's fill the room.

I believe grief is the shadow of love. It's made of the same stuff. I'm learning to carry mine like a coat.

I believe in kitchens. In ordinary rooms. In the right to a quiet Tuesday.

And I believe that I've changed, and that it isn't over. I don't know who I'll be at home. I'd like to be gentler. A better husband, a better father, a man who notices the bread.

About Katya: Olha gave me homework. I did it three days ago. I called, on a good line, and I said: "I need to tell you something, and I want you to just listen." She said "Okay." I told her about the counting. About why I do it. About the room in the dugout where the ground moves. About Bohdan, whom I'd mentioned only as *a friend,* and about Zenko, and the night.

She didn't speak for almost a minute. Then she said, in a voice I'd never heard, "I've been counting too."

"What?"

"In the corridor. Since the first month. Every night. I do it with Sonya under the blanket. I never told you." A breath. "I thought you'd hate that I'd learned."

We stayed on the line for an hour. Neither of us said anything clever. At the end she said, "So we count together, now. That's the new thing."

I don't believe all of this every day. Some days I only believe in socks. But I've written it here in pencil so I can read it on the bad days and find out who I was when I was trying.

Motor read over my shoulder this morning, silently, like a very large owl. "Add one," he said.

"What?"

"*The kettle goes to the line.*"

"That's Vira's position."

"I know," said Motor. "I'm trying to start a peace."

# Day 87 — Notes: What Changed

Before: courage is loud. Now: courage is quiet.
Before: be self-reliant. Now: be generous, and accept help.
Before: fear is shameful. Now: fear is information.
Before: home is a place. Now: home is people, and a corridor with a good wall.
