# Day 70 — Dispatch: What We Don't Say

We don't talk about politics much, which surprises people.

They imagine fiery debates in lamplight, fists on tables. There are some. But more often politics arrives sideways, as a sigh, a joke, a long silence after the news.

Last night it arrived as Yaroslav. He'd been listening to something on his phone, a clip of a man in a good suit making a promise, and took out one earbud and said flatly, "I wonder how many of them have ever carried water."

Nobody answered. Motor's mustache twitched.

"It's not fair," said Vira at last. "They have a different job."

"I know."

"It's a hard job."

"I know it is." He scratched his neck. "I just wish they knew what it was like. Even once. For a day."

Zenko closed his book. "May I offer a small observation? I taught civics, for a year, before history. The one thing I'm sure of is that every government has been out of touch with its soldiers. Rome. Byzantium. The Cossack Hetmans. It's practically a law of nature."

"That doesn't make it okay."

"No. It makes it permanent. The question isn't whether we're forgotten by those above. The question is what we build below."

Halyna, dry: "Soup, mostly."

"Soup is political," said Zenko. "Soup is the most political thing there is."

I won't tell you what each of us thinks. It varies. We come from different towns, different families, different loyalties. One of us voted one way, one another, one not at all. Motor says he doesn't vote, he only fixes things. But when the shells fall nobody asks who you voted for. When a man's bleeding no one checks his manifesto.

There's a kind of politics that's only about the person next to you. Do they have water? Are they warm? Did they sleep? Have they been heard? It's smaller and more honest than the other kind.

Late, I went out, as I do. Yaroslav was at the post with his phone dark in his pocket. We stood a while. At last he said, "Bohdan used to say we shouldn't be bitter."

"Did he?"

"He said the other side wasn't an idea, it was a lot of people with a bad boss. I didn't understand. I do a bit more now."

"And?"

"I don't want to be bitter." He looked at the dark field. "But I think I'm going to be, for a while."

"Then be bitter. But be bitter with your boots on."

He laughed, thinly. "That's not Plutarch."

"It's Motor."

# Day 70 — Notes: Trust

I trust the person next to me. The driver who brings the water. The nurse who checks my feet.

I want from those above: honesty even when it's bad, competence, plain and boring and reliable, presence, fairness in leave and praise, and memory. Not to forget us when it's over.
