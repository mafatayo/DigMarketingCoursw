# Day 70 — Dispatch: Water

"I wonder," Yaroslav said, with one earbud out, "how many of them have ever carried water."

Nobody answered. There was a clip playing on his phone, a man in a good suit making a promise, and the tiny tinny sound of it filled the dugout for a second until he turned it off. Motor's mustache twitched.

"It's not fair," Vira said at last. "They have a different job."

"I know."

"It's a hard job."

"I know it is." He scratched his neck. "I just wish they knew what it was like. Even once. For a day."

Zenko closed his book. "May I offer a small observation? I taught civics before history. The one thing I'm sure of is that every government has been out of touch with its soldiers. Rome. Byzantium. The Cossack Hetmans. It's practically a law of nature."

"That doesn't make it okay."

"No. It makes it permanent. The question isn't whether we're forgotten by the people above. It's what we build below."

"Soup, mostly," said Halyna.

"Soup is political," said Zenko. "Soup is the most political thing there is."

I won't tell you what each of us thinks. We come from different towns, different families. One voted one way, one another, one not at all. Motor says he doesn't vote, he fixes things. But when the shells fall nobody asks who you voted for, and when a man's bleeding nobody checks his manifesto.

At about midnight I went out and Yaroslav was at the post with the phone dark in his hand. We stood a bit.

"Bohdan used to say we shouldn't get bitter," he said.

"Did he?"

"He said the other side wasn't an idea. It was a lot of people with a bad boss. I didn't understand then. I understand a bit more." He looked at the dark field. "I don't want to be bitter."

"Good."

"I think I'm going to be, for a while."

"Then be bitter with your boots on."

He laughed, thinly. "That's not Plutarch."

"It's Motor."
