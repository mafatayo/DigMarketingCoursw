# Day 81 — Dispatch: Monday

**Bottom line:** A veteran asked what we'll do on the Monday after the war, and I wrote my first list.

A man came to visit from the rear, a veteran of an earlier phase of all this, with a prosthetic leg and a stick, and he asked us the question none of us could answer.

Ihor. About forty-five, broad, balding, with the quiet humorous bearing of someone who's made peace with a great deal. He'd come to talk to the new men, he said, "just to chat." We sat on crates in a circle and he told stories. Some funny. A few not. At one point he rapped his leg with his knuckle and it gave a hollow knock. "This one's called Boris. Boris and I have an understanding."

Then, near the end, he looked round the circle. "Here's what nobody asks until it's too late. When it's over, what will you *do?*"

Silence. Even the Colonel seemed to listen.

"I mean it. Not what you'll feel. What you'll do on a Monday. At nine in the morning. When you wake in your own bed and nobody's telling you where to go."

I think we'd each assumed that the question was never going to be ours. That the war would end, and life would resume, by some obscure magic, exactly where we'd left it.

"I was a welder," Ihor said. "I assumed I'd weld. Then my hands didn't like the heat. Then my back didn't like the angle. Then I realized I had no idea who I was without the war or the welding. I'm fine now. I teach apprentices. But it took two years of being miserable to find out."

"What would you tell us?" said Yaroslav.

"Start thinking now. Not planning. Thinking. What did you like as a boy, the kind of thing that made time vanish? Where do you feel useful? Write it down. Keep the list in a pocket."

The conversation drifted. But I saw the effect all week. Zenko, drafting what might be the first page of a book. Vira asking Motor whether he'd ever thought about running a bus depot, and Motor laughing so hard he had to wipe his eyes. Halyna saying she'd like "a very small clinic in a very small village with a very large garden."

And Yaroslav asking whether anybody knew what you did for the part of the list that was about someone else. He didn't say who. We all knew. Ihor, who'd stayed on for tea, looked at him over his cup.

"You carry it," he said. "That's all. I carry mine. It gets lighter. It doesn't leave."

I wrote my own list, the first line trembling a little. *Sound. Quiet. A room that's mine.* And under it, after a long while: *Kitchen. Eggs. Third stair.* I don't know what it means yet. But it's mine, and I've put it in my pocket.

# Day 81 — Notes: Monday

**Bottom line:** Write the plan for Monday now.

When it's over there'll be a Monday. A city to rebuild, a half-generation of children who've learned to listen for planes. Veterans looking for work. Widows looking for answers.

I'd like the plan for Monday written now, by people who've been in the basement.
