# Day 53 — Dispatch: The Ceasefire

**Bottom line:** A false ceasefire rumor made me want to believe it more than anyone, and the shelling got worse that night.

The rumor came on a Tuesday, and by Wednesday it had legs, a beard, and a pension.

It began as a whisper that the fighting in a far region had ended. By noon it was a ceasefire. By evening an agreement. By night someone in the next dugout swore he'd heard on the radio that we'd all be home by the weekend. A man in a neighboring position wept with joy. A second began to pack.

We heard it the way you hear a bell far off, faint and distorted and pleasant. Yaroslav ran in with his eyes enormous. "Is it true?"

"Who told you?"

"Taras told me. Taras heard from Oleh. Oleh said the driver said."

"The driver of what?"

"A truck."

"That," said Zenko, with the weary patience of a man who has seen forty thousand years of rumor, "is not a source. That is a *vehicle.*"

I watched it spread through the position like water through cloth. It's astonishing how a story grows. Every retelling adds a detail. By the fifth, it had acquired a date, a treaty, a signature and, inexplicably, a banquet.

Motor held a meeting. He sat us in a row and spoke in his flat solid voice. "Maybe it's true. Maybe not. Nothing has changed for us. We have our jobs. We're here until someone with real authority tells us otherwise. Until then: stove lit, watch kept."

"Yes, Sergeant."

"And if someone tells you something, ask three questions. Who said it? How do they know? What do they want?"

"What do they want?" said Yaroslav.

"Everyone wants something."

I wanted to believe it more than any of them. I'd said nothing, I'd stayed calm. But in the middle of the second night I'd found myself lying awake composing the call to Katya. *It's over, come to the station.* I could see her face. I could see Sonya on the platform. It was a very clear picture and it took me an hour to put it down.

The rumor died next morning, the way they do. A supply officer came through and shrugged. "No ceasefire. Not that I know of." And a soft collective sag went through the position, the quiet slump of men who'd allowed themselves a day of hope.

Nobody was angry. Yaroslav said, "It was nice for a day, though," and Vira said, "It was." I think that was the whole of it. A rumor is a kind of dream. It tells you what you want. And for a day, we'd gotten to want it out loud.

That night, though, the shelling was worse than it had been in a week. As if the other side had decided to correct a misunderstanding.

**Tactical read.** Rumor is an information weapon. Units counter it by demanding sources and waiting for official confirmation.

**OPSEC.** Frequencies, call procedures and signal details are withheld.

# Day 53 — Notes: Ending It

**Bottom line:** We want it to end so our children never have to fight it again.

Everyone here wants it to stop. Nobody's allowed to say it first; it sounds like surrender. So we say it quietly, over tea.

What we mean is: we want it to end in a way our children don't have to fight again. We want it to end because it's finished, not because someone's tired.

*Dispatches from the Line is a work of fiction.*
