# Day 51 — Dispatch: How Is It Really

Katya asked me a direct question last night, and I lied.

It was a good lie. Warm, practical, well constructed. I've been practicing for weeks. She said, over a poor line, with the crackle of distance in her voice, "Danylo, how is it really?"

"It's fine. It's quiet. We're just waiting."

A pause. Long. I could hear our kitchen behind her. The tap that doesn't drip anymore. A pan on the stove. Sonya singing something tuneless in the next room.

"You're lying," she said gently.

"No."

"You've lost weight in your voice."

I laughed, then stopped. "Katya."

"I know you can't tell me things. I'm not asking for things. I'm asking if you're all right."

I stood at the post in the cold with the phone against my cheek and my breath fogging. I wanted so badly to tell her. About the name said at the table. About the notebook. About a ring of clumsy hands. About how I'd stood forty feet from a friend and couldn't walk. I wanted to put every bit of it on the line and let it run to her kitchen.

I didn't. I want you to understand why, because I think it's the most common mistake we make.

If I tell her, she'll carry it. She'll carry it into the night, to the bakery, to the school gate, to her mother's table. She'll hold it because she loves me and she'll never put it down. I'd be handing her a stone across two hundred kilometers and she couldn't do one thing about it. And she's already carrying so much. She's carrying the corridor.

So I gave her a smaller true thing.

"I'm tired. More than I've ever been. I'm frightened sometimes. A friend of mine died. I won't tell you more than that. I'm with good people. They look after me. I've got a dog and a mug and a woman who reads dragon stories. I'm all right, Katya. Not fine. All right."

She was silent. Then, very softly: "Thank you."

"For what?"

"For giving me the real size of it."

We stayed on the line without speaking. I listened to her breathe. In the next room Sonya had stopped singing. I thought I heard her say "Mama?" and then, in the middle of the word, a siren started, thin and rising, on her end, a sound I'd have known anywhere. "I have to go," Katya said, in a different voice, the quick flat one. "Love you. Eat."

And the line cut.

I stood there a long while with the dead phone against my ear. Four hundred kilometers away my wife was picking up our child and walking to a corridor, and I was holding a piece of plastic, and I couldn't even stand up straight.

# Day 51 — Notes: Edits

I don't say the names of places. I don't say the names of the hurt. I don't say how close it got. I don't say what the nights are like. I don't say I've written a letter "in case."

I do say that I ate, that I'm warm, that the dog likes me. I say I love them, every call, once, plainly.

There's a difference between lying and carrying. I'm not always sure which side of it I'm on.
