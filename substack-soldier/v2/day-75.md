*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 75 — Dispatch: Thaw

It begins with a smell.

I was outside at dawn with the kettle and the air had changed. Not warmer, exactly. Softer. There was something in it, a damp richness, like turned earth, like the first moment you open a door on a garden. I stood still with my face lifted until the kettle shrieked.

"What is it?" said Halyna, coming up behind me, wrapped in her enormous coat.

"Spring," I said. "I think."

"Don't say that."

"Why?"

"It jinxes it."

But we both smelled it. Under the cold, the ghost of something green.

By afternoon, dripping. A steady plink from the eaves of the dugout, each drop catching the pale sun. The snow in the fields had gone gray and sagging with dark patches showing through, like the skin of an old animal shedding its coat. A single crow stood on the nearest patch, looking gravely at the mud, as if reading a contract.

The rumors, I should tell you, begin at the same time. Every thaw is accompanied by whispering. This year it's different. It isn't about peace. It's about something being gathered, on the far side. Trucks seen at night. A rumor of numbers. Vira has been listening harder on the net than she lets on, and last night she said to Motor, low, "The jamming's changed." That's all. He nodded and went out.

Here's what I've learned about spring and war: they are old and unpleasant bedfellows. Winter's a kind of pause. A frozen breath held by all sides. The thaw means movement, and movement means the thing they've been saving.

Nobody has said it. But everyone's started sleeping with boots on again, even the ones who'd got lax. Halyna has doubled the count on the shelf. Motor has been out to walk the line three times in two days and comes back with his lips pressed flat.

And yet. I stood on the sandbags tonight and watched the light go. The sky was the pale green of a young leaf. A bird, the first I'd heard in weeks, tried a phrase, got it wrong, tried again.

I felt it rise in me, treacherous and sweet. Hope. That unreliable guest. I let it in. I let it sit at my table for the length of a single song.

Then Motor from the steps: "Lys. Water."

And I went.

# Day 75 — Notes: Hope

Dangerous because it makes you careless, or bitter if it's dashed. Necessary because without it nobody gets up.

I carry it lightly, like an egg. Small hopes: tomorrow's tea, next week's mail, the first green shoot by the sandbags.
