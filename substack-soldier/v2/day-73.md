*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 73 — Dispatch: A Pallet

A pallet arrived with a flag on the side that wasn't ours and the whole position gathered to look at it.

A squared-off block of boxes wrapped in plastic, strapped down, stenciled in a language none of us could read, with a blue-and-yellow sticker slapped on one corner by someone's cheerful hand. The driver beside it, exhausted, filling in a form on a clipboard.

"What is it?" whispered Yaroslav.

"Let's open it," said Vira.

It took a while to peel the plastic. When Motor cut the straps, the top box fell open and out came, gloriously, vacuum-packed rolls of thermal underwear, each with a tag. And a handwritten note on top in careful English: *From the people of a small town you may never have heard of. We think of you. Stay warm.*

"I want to meet them," said Halyna.

Underneath: proper medical kits in sturdy pouches. Boxes of gloves. Dry rations in colored foil. A case of the batteries we'd been short of. A sack of woolen hats.

We spent the afternoon sorting in the sharp bright chill with the Colonel inspecting each box. At some point Zenko, who'd been studying the stenciled alphabet, said, "It's from a country I've never visited and a place I couldn't find on a map. And here are warm socks."

You'd think aid is about goods. The goods are almost the least of it. A pallet from far away says: *you aren't alone.* A man in a hole at the end of the world looks at a stenciled box in a strange alphabet and realizes an ocean of people he'll never meet has decided to care. In the long winter, loneliness is a more dangerous enemy than the cold.

There's frustration too. We hear about promises, delays, long debates in distant rooms. What we need most sometimes comes last. What we need least comes first. Bohdan once received eleven crates of batteries nothing here uses.

The pallet didn't have everything. I looked for it. Motor looked for it. Under the hats there was a small case of the one item that mattered most, and it was marked *limited,* and when Motor counted it he did the sum, and I watched his lips move. He said nothing. But he looked up at the sky and I knew what he was counting, which was nights.

Then he lifted a woolen hat from the sack, turned it over in his big hands, and put it on. It was too small. It sat on top of his head like a cherry on a cake. He regarded us without expression.

"Not a word," he said.

# Day 73 — Notes: Support

What helps most: people who show up, steady supplies, honest information, training and tools, solidarity, not forgetting.

Keep going. Don't get bored. Don't believe the rumors that we're alone.
