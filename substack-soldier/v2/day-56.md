# Day 56 — Dispatch: 5:40

The best hour of my day is 5:40 a.m., and I'll defend it.

It's the gap, not the sleep, which I'm always short of. By 5:40 the night watch is ending, the day watch hasn't started, and a thin crack opens in the clock. Everyone's half awake. Nobody's had to be anything yet.

I get up before the others, most days. My back wakes me. I lie there listening to the dugout breathe: Motor's slow snore, Vira's tiny whistle, the click of the stove cooling. Then I pull on my boots, careful not to wake the Colonel, who sleeps on my feet like a hot water bottle with opinions, and take the kettle outside.

The cold at that hour is different. Cleaner. It smells of iron and frost and woodsmoke from some distant stove. The sky's the color of a dove's chest, gray going to pink along one edge. Every blade of grass has white fur.

I light the burner behind the sandbags, shielding it with my body, and wait. The metal ticks. A small blue flame hisses. I hold my hands over it the way you do over a campfire and my fingers slowly come back to me.

When the water boils I make the first tea. Two bags, a spoon of sugar. I pour a mug and stand there with the steam on my face, looking east.

This is the part I hold on to: the first sip, heat sliding down and spreading in the chest like a small sun. A few birds start, tentative, tuning. Somewhere a dog barks once and thinks better of it.

Since Bohdan, it's changed. There's a place on the sandbags to my left where he used to stand at this hour, with his machine, doing the dawn check. I haven't decided what to do with it. Today I poured two mugs, out of habit, and stood there with them, and then drank one and set the other on the sandbag beside me.

Halyna came up. She didn't speak. She stood next to me and looked at the second mug, and then took it, and drank it, and neither of us said anything.

Then Motor, hands in his pockets, mustache frosted. I gave him tea. He drank, grunted, and after a minute said, "Good tea."

"Thanks."

"I'll hum about it."

And he did, softly, the four bars drifting out across the frozen field. The sun came up while we stood there. Pink, gold, white. And then, as it does, the day.

# Day 56 — Notes: Opening

The bakery in the town opens at six. It opened the week the glass went, and the week after the shelling, and the week the flour was short. The woman who runs it says she can't stand to think of someone coming for bread and the door being locked.

She's the most important person in the city.
