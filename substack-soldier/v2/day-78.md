# Day 78 — Dispatch: The Lieutenant

**Bottom line:** Our young lieutenant was wounded, and Yaroslav tied the tourniquet under his orders.

Maksym was hit on a Wednesday, and I'm going to tell it in the order I understood it, which wasn't the order it happened.

I understood first that something was wrong with the quiet. It was a clear bright morning, the larks up, and then the larks stopped. All at once. In the way Zenko says birds do. We all looked up. I understood second that Halyna was already running.

He'd gone out at first light to look at a stretch of the line himself. He does that. He's learned. He goes out in the morning with Motor's old binoculars and walks the section, not to give orders, just to see. He'd told us where he was going. Motor had said, "Take someone." Maksym had said, "Next time." It was the sort of thing a boy says.

It was close, whatever it was. I heard the crack and the long whine after it, and I was on the ground before I'd decided to be, the Colonel's body flattened beside mine. When I raised my head the air was full of a brown haze and somebody was shouting.

It was Yaroslav, from the trench. "Lieutenant! *Lieutenant!*"

I found out later what it was. A piece of something, nothing dramatic, a fragment the size of a coin. It had gone through his upper arm and then, on its way, taken part of his shoulder. He was conscious the whole time. He was sitting in the trench with his back against the wall and his face gray and his left arm wrong in a way I'll leave out, and he was giving Yaroslav instructions in a steady voice.

"Put pressure here. No. Harder. You can't hurt it. Do the tourniquet, you know how, Halyna showed you. Do the time."

"I don't—"

"You do. Do it."

He did. Nineteen years old. His hands were shaking so hard he dropped the strap twice. Maksym waited each time, patient, and said, "Again. Good. Now the time. Write it."

Halyna arrived and took over without a word and I saw Yaroslav sit back on his heels, covered in blood to the elbows, and look at his own hands like they were somebody else's.

They took the lieutenant out on a stretcher, with Oksana, the girl with the ponytail, driving. He kept talking the whole way. At the van he caught Motor's sleeve. "Sergeant. I'm sorry. I should've taken someone."

"Yes," said Motor.

"Next time."

"Yes," said Motor. He held the boy's good hand a second. "There will be a next time, Lieutenant. You'll do it right."

The van went out. We stood in the brown haze with the larks silent, and nobody spoke until Motor lit the stove.

He'll live. I know that because we heard that evening. He'll lose some use of the arm. He'll be a long time in the ward. He was twenty-four and he'd been here forty days.

Yaroslav washed his hands at the bucket for twenty minutes. I counted. Then Motor went and stood next to him, not speaking, and handed him the soap.

**Tactical read.** Leader casualties are normal in forward positions. Deputy and NCO depth is what keeps a section running.

**OPSEC.** Medical details and casualty identities are limited to what a family would already know.

# Day 78 — Notes: What a Lieutenant Costs

**Bottom line:** "Not life-threatening" isn't what the mother hears.

A young man's arm. The use of it. Maksym will be fine, I'm told, in the way that people say it.

Someone's mother will be told it's "not life-threatening." She'll hear everything else.
