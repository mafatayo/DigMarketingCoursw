# Day 74 — Dispatch: Weather

Zenko asked me something strange at dinner. "When did you last see a report that made you feel seen?"

I thought about it. "I can't remember."

"Neither can I." He pushed a bean across his plate. "I'm starting to think we've become weather."

I knew what he meant. Coverage has thinned. The long front-page pieces shrank to short columns, then a line, then a ticker at the bottom of a screen. People who once checked the news hourly check it weekly. We've become background noise, like traffic: always there, sometimes bad, mostly unremarked.

I don't blame them. If I lived at home I'd tire too. There's a limit to how much heartbreak a body can hold. The world has its own wars and troubles and bills.

But from here it feels strange. A little like waving from a distant ship. You see the faces on the shore getting smaller and think: surely they'll look up again.

Vira was fierce about it. "I don't need to be famous. I need to be *remembered.* There's a difference."

"What's the difference?" said Yaroslav.

"If you're famous, you're a story. If you're remembered, you're a person."

"I'd take remembered," said Halyna.

"I'd take dry boots," said Motor.

We laughed, but it stayed with me. And I'll tell you the part I don't like. Since Bohdan I've been catching myself keeping score. *They have a screen, we have a hole.* *They have a Tuesday.* I know it's unfair. It rises in me like acid, at night, and I don't invite it. I think it's what happens to a person who is far from home and tired and has put a name in a notebook.

So I've made a rule. I won't be angry at the tired. I'll tell the small true things. A dog. A mug. A carol over the radio. If one person somewhere stops scrolling for a minute and thinks, *oh, they're still there,* I count it as victory.

You're here. That's the proof. Thank you.

I said that last part more quietly than I meant, to the page, in the dark, and the Colonel opened one eye.

# Day 74 — Notes: Forgotten

To the tired: it's okay. You don't have to feel it all the time. Do one thing.

To the ones who keep reading: you matter more than you know.

To anyone who feels forgotten, in any war, any illness, any lonely room: you exist. Someone is thinking of you.
