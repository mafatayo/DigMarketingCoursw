*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 11 — Dispatch: Shower Day

Eleven days. I'd stopped noticing the smell around day six, which Halyna says is the dangerous stage.

The word came at breakfast: a mobile shower, a truck with a tank and a boiler and a tarp, set up at a rear position. Ninety minutes, groups of three, somebody had to stay on the line. Motor read a list. Mine was the first group. I stood up so fast I hit my head on the beam.

"He'll come back smelling of roses and shame," said Vira.

The ride was forty minutes in the back of a pickup between jerrycans, over every pothole in the country, with a plastic bag in my lap holding a towel and a bar of soap Katya had sent and a clean shirt I'd been saving for a day I didn't know the name of. Three of us. Nobody talked. At one point the road ran past a village, or what had been one, and I looked at it like a stranger's accident, trying not to, and failing. A school. The front wall was off, so you could see the classrooms stacked like shelves, one with a map still pinned up. A swing set stood intact in the yard.

What I thought wasn't noble. I'm going to have a shower. In front of that. And I was still looking forward to it, and I felt like a pig.

The shower was a blue tarp on poles with wooden pallets for a floor. A man in a knitted hat ran the boiler and shouted the time. The water came from a bucket with holes punched in it, and when it hit my shoulders I made a sound that still embarrasses me. The man in the hat had heard it before and didn't look up.

It ran gray, then brown, then, slowly, clear. I stood there with my eyes shut and thought about nothing. For about four minutes I was a person with no past and no future, only warmth and a bar of soap that smelled like lemon.

"Ten minutes! Let the next one in!"

I put on the clean shirt. It smelled like Katya's closet. That nearly finished me. I stood by the truck with my hair dripping and a stupid look on my face, and a big quiet man from another unit held out a cigarette. I don't smoke. I took it. We stood there not saying anything, two clean men in the cold.

On the way back the light went gold across the fields, and somebody was asleep against my arm. I watched the school go past again. The map was still on the wall.

When I came into the dugout Vira sniffed the air and said, "Lemon. Fine. Lys can sit at the table."

# Day 11 — Notes: Hot Water

One shower: about forty liters, an hour of boiler time, a driver, a day, a risk. Thirty men a day if everything goes well. So ten minutes under that bucket is somebody's entire morning.

At home I used to stand under the tap for twenty minutes thinking about email.

If someone offers to wash your clothes, say yes. Don't be proud.
