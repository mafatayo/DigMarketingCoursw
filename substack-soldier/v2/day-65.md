# Day 65 — Dispatch: Seven Days

The officer who came in with the leave order brought snow on his cap and read from a sheet, and Zenko was in the middle of explaining the Treaty of Hadiach.

"Corporal Lys. Seven days. Tomorrow, six a.m., from the junction. Truck, then a train."

Nobody moved. I thought I'd misheard.

"Lys," said Motor. "That's you."

"I know it's me."

"So say something."

I opened my mouth and what came out was, "Who'll feed the Colonel?"

They laughed until Halyna had to put down her mug. Vira said, "He's been given leave to go home and he's worried about a dog." Zenko said the dog was a better soldier than any of us and was he possibly in line for a decoration.

I packed in the dark. A clean shirt. The notebook. Three of the children's cards for Sonya. A rough little carving Motor had made, a fox no bigger than a thumb, which he handed over without looking at me. "For the girl. Tell her it's from the sergeant."

Then I lay on my bunk with my heart going like a drum. I was imagining the door. The stairs. The creak on the third step. And under it there was another thought that I'd rather not have written down but I will. I was the one going. Halyna hadn't had leave since before I came. Yaroslav's mother hadn't seen him in months. A quiet voice in me said, take it, don't ask whose turn, and I lay there feeling a thin cold shame and, underneath, a hunger so large I was afraid of it.

At the junction the truck was late. A handful of us stood in the snow with our bags, quiet, half terrified, the way people look in a waiting room. The man next to me was bouncing on the balls of his feet. Very young, a mustache that hadn't committed.

"My mother doesn't know," he said, not looking at me. "I'm going to knock and say, 'Surprise.'"

"She'll faint."

"I hope so."

"She might hit you."

"That too," he said. "I'd take that."

The truck came round the bend with one headlight out.

# Day 65 — Notes: What Leave Costs

Leave isn't only for the one who goes. It's a week of cooking, a week of tidying, a week of pretending you're not counting. And then a morning with a bag on the floor, and a child holding a rabbit.

Whoever invented the leave pass should be asked to spend the last day.
