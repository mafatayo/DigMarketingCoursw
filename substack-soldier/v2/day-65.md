*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 65 — Dispatch: Seven Days

The leave order came on a Monday in the middle of a lecture from Zenko on the Treaty of Hadiach.

A young officer ducked in shaking snow off his cap and read from a sheet in the tone of a man who knows he's about to make someone's year. "Corporal Lys. Seven days. Departure tomorrow, six a.m., from the junction. Truck, then a train."

I didn't move either. I was fairly sure I'd misheard.

"Lys," said Motor. "That's you."

"I know it's me."

"Then say something."

I opened my mouth. What came out was, "Who'll feed the Colonel?"

The whole dugout broke into laughter. Halyna put down her mug to wipe her eyes. Vira said, "He's been given leave to go home and he's worried about a dog." "That dog," said Zenko solemnly, "is a better soldier than any of us."

I packed that night in a daze, which didn't take long. A clean shirt. The notebook. Three of the children's cards for Sonya. A small rough carving Motor had made me, a fox, no bigger than a thumb, handed over without ceremony. "For the girl. Tell her it's from the sergeant."

I couldn't sleep. I lay in the dark with my heart going like a drum imagining it: the door, the stairs, the third step that creaks. Would the flat smell the same? Would Sonya be shy? Would I be the same man who left?

There was another thought, under it, and I'll write it down because I promised honesty. I'm the one who goes. Seven days. Nobody else in the section is going. Halyna hasn't had leave since before I came. Yaroslav's mother hasn't seen him in months. And a quiet voice in me said: *take it, take it, don't ask whose turn.* I felt, in the dark, a thin cold shame, and underneath it a hunger so large I was afraid of it.

At the junction the truck was late. I stood in the snow with my bag among a handful of other soldiers going on leave, all silent, all half terrified. A very young man next to me was bouncing on the balls of his feet. "My mother doesn't know. I'm going to knock on the door and say, 'Surprise.'"

"She'll faint."

"I hope so," he said. "I really do."

When the truck came I climbed up without looking back. Then, at the last second, I did. In the gray dawn, small and square against the white, a figure stood on the far rise. A tall silhouette with a very big mustache and a small dog at his feet.

He didn't wave. He just stood. I watched until the truck turned the corner.

# Day 65 — Notes: Before

Afraid of: not being recognized, not recognizing them, flinching at a car door, being too quiet, not wanting to go back, wanting to go back.

Hoping for: a hot bath. The creak of the third stair. Katya's hand. Sonya on my shoulders. My mother awake and difficult.

The others told me: don't plan, just be there.
