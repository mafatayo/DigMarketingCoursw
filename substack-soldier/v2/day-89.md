*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 89 — Dispatch: The Questions

I've been saving the questions, and tonight, with the lamp low and the stove ticking, I'll answer a few properly. Vira's reading them in her best radio voice. Halyna insists on tea first.

**How do you keep going on the days you don't want to?**

You don't keep going. You do the next thing. That's a different muscle. A kettle. A bucket. A letter. Nobody has to want to. They just have to start, and the wanting sometimes shows up later like a late guest.

**What's the funniest thing that's happened?**

Hard to choose. The callsign. The wrench in the bread bin. The day Yaroslav apologized to a hedgehog, which I think he picked up from someone. But I think the winner is the night the whole radio net sang in dreadful harmony and the old officer in the north said "Is that a choir or an air raid?" and Vira, without pausing, said, "Both, sir."

**Is it true soldiers don't talk about the hard things?**

Partly. We talk sideways. We say "bad day." We pass each other tea. The hard things come out in odd corners, in a joke, a silence, a hand on a shoulder. You have to learn to hear them.

**What would you say to someone thinking of joining?**

Be honest with yourself about why. Learn first aid. Learn to sleep anywhere. Pack wool. Find a few good people. And call home, even when you can't tell them anything.

**What's the most important thing you've learned?**

That the good people outnumber the others by a wide margin. I'd have bet otherwise on Day 1.

**Do you ever think about quitting?**

Constantly. I don't, because of the faces. If I left, someone else would have to carry my bucket.

**Does the dog know?**

I believe he does. I believe he knows exactly what's going on and has decided to be calm on all our behalf.

Halyna has asked me to add one more. *Wash your hands.* She says she has received no questions on the subject and is concerned.
