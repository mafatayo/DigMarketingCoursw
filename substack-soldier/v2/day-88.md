*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 88 — Dispatch: To Someone Who's Never Been

You've asked me, in different ways, for three months, what it's really like. Here's my best attempt, with the understanding that it'll fail.

Imagine the loudest day of your life, a day when something enormous happened. Now imagine it's over. And then imagine a hundred days of waiting for it to happen again. Most of it is that. A long wait, in the cold, with people you've come to love, doing small jobs.

Imagine being so tired you put salt in your tea and drink it.

Imagine a kettle as the center of your moral world.

Imagine learning to tell, by sound, which of the noises overhead is meant for you. Imagine getting it right, and what that does to you.

Imagine laughing in the worst possible place, and nobody thinking less of you.

Imagine finding that your grandmother was right about dill.

Imagine a dog who leans.

Imagine a list of names in a notebook that grows faster than you wanted.

Imagine a boy, twenty-three, who apologized to every machine he lost. Imagine standing forty feet from him and not being able to walk.

Imagine a woman you've never met getting into a car and driving seven hours to put a bowl of soup in your hands, and refusing to give her name.

Imagine being scared, and doing the next thing.

Imagine missing someone so much it's a physical object, a stone in your pocket, and you wouldn't put it down if you could.

Imagine being good at something you never expected to be good at, and wishing with your whole heart you'd never had to learn it.

That's the closest I can come. If you're someone who's never been, who watches from a distance: it isn't a failure of yours. Don't feel guilty. Don't feel you owe a performance of suffering. Stay human. Stay curious. Stay kind. Be a person who, if it ever came to it, would carry the water.

# Day 88 — Notes: What They Ask

*Are you afraid?* Yes, every day.
*Do you hate them?* I try not to think about hate. It's heavy. I think about the person next to me. Some nights I do hate them, cleanly, for about a minute.
*What do you miss most?* Doors. Quiet. My daughter's feet.
*What do you eat?* Buckwheat. Pickles. What somebody's grandmother sent.
*How can I help?* Be steady. Give a little, regularly. Don't look away.
*Will you come back?* I'm working on it. It's the only plan I've got.
