# Day 47 — Dispatch: A Good Day

**Bottom line:** A plain good day, which I wrote down because I've learned what the next one can cost.

I'm going to describe a good day, because we need one on record, and because it's hard to do. Good days leave no marks.

It began with a thin clear sun through a hole in the cloud, lying along the frost like a blade. The cold was dry and bright. The Colonel stood on the sandbags and barked at nothing with great importance.

Breakfast was eggs. Real ones, from a village woman who wouldn't take payment. Halyna fried them with a little onion in a pan on the stove, and the smell was so good that Zenko stopped mid-sentence and closed his eyes. We ate in silence, each of us in a small private church.

At nine the radio was quiet. Vira put on a song, very low, a woman's voice, old and sweet, and hummed. Motor fixed a boot at the table with Yaroslav beside him learning how. Bohdan flew a short check and came back grinning. "Beautiful. You can see for miles."

At eleven I went out with Zenko for water. The walk was cold and pleasant and slow. He told me about a student who'd once written an essay on the Cossacks that was, in its entirety, three sentences: *They rode. They fought. They sang.* "Perfect," said Zenko. "Full marks."

At noon the mail came. Two letters for me. Katya's was short and silly, about the neighbor's cat opening a window. Sonya's was a drawing of me with enormous hands and my own face at last, no mustache. *It's you,* she'd written, in giant careful letters. *I know now.*

In the afternoon I slept an hour on my bunk with the Colonel's chin on my knee, a deep dreamless sleep.

At dusk six of us sat in a row on the sandbags with mugs while the sky turned violet and the first star came out. Nobody talked. A bird went by. Far off something rumbled and none of us turned our heads. Bohdan, on my left, had the green notebook on his knee and was drawing, very small and neat, in the margin, a little fox. He showed me without a word and I gave him a thumbs up.

That was all. That was a good day. Warm eggs, a clear sky, the shared quiet of people who like each other.

I wrote it down so I'd remember. If you ever wonder what we're fighting for, I think it's days like that, in as many kitchens as possible, for as long as possible, and not flags or maps.

When I put out the lamp that night I thought: *that was a very good day, and I should hold onto it.* I didn't know why. I just had the feeling you get when you've been given a gift and haven't been told the price.

# Day 47 — Notes: A Good Day, Elsewhere

**Bottom line:** The good day we're fighting for belongs to someone else.

Somewhere, tonight, a woman is cooking soup in a flat with the lights on, and her children are asleep. That's the good day, the one we're fighting for. Not mine. Hers.

I'd like her never to know my name.

*Dispatches from the Line is a work of fiction.*
