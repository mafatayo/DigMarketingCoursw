# Day 36 — Dispatch: Smell

Nobody tells you fear has a smell, so let me correct the record.

It's metallic. Sharp, a little sour, like a coin held under the tongue or the inside of a cold pipe. It comes off the body. I noticed it first in the van, on the night we carried him, and thought it was the stretcher. Then it was in the dugout the next morning, hanging in the air. Then I smelled it on my own sleeve and understood it was mine.

Halyna has a theory that the body knows. "Animals smell it. The Colonel can. People can, too, they just don't admit it."

The Colonel was a better barometer than any of us. For two days after the night drive he wouldn't leave Bohdan's side. Bohdan hadn't said much since. He sat with his back against the wall and knees drawn up, staring at nothing, and the dog leaned against his shin.

I sat down next to him. I didn't talk. After a while he said, "I thought I'd be fine."

"You were fine."

"I held the stretcher. I didn't drop it."

"You didn't drop it."

"But I couldn't feel my hands. After. I thought they'd come off. I thought, those aren't mine."

I nodded, I'd had the same thing, and I told him.

"How do you stop it?"

"I don't think you stop it. I think you learn what it is." I rubbed my palms together. "Zenko said something once. He said fear isn't the opposite of courage. It's the price. Every brave thing costs some fear and you're just paying the bill."

"That's a very teacher thing to say."

"He's a teacher."

Bohdan nearly smiled. We sat in the faint sour smell with the dog breathing between us.

That night neither of us slept. At two he whispered from the bunk across from mine, "Lys? Are you scared right now?"

"Yes."

"Good," he said. "Me too. I just wanted to check it wasn't only me."

There was a thing I didn't say. I'd looked at him in the light of the lamp, the thin neck and the pale wrists, and thought, with a clarity that felt like cold water, that he was the one they'd go for. He flew, and that was reason enough. Every unit on this front understands that the man at the screen is the first target. I'd known it as a fact. Now it had a face.

I lay there and didn't say any of it.

# Day 36 — Notes: The Siren

A girl in a shelter asked me whether the sirens were angry. I said no. She asked whether they were sad. I said maybe. She nodded and said, "I think they're scared. That's why they're so loud."

I haven't heard one the same way since.
