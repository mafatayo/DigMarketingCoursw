*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 55 — Dispatch: The List

I've been keeping a list.

It started on Day 48, when the name was said at the table and I was ashamed to find I had no way to hold it. I'd told myself I'd remember. Memory's a leaky bucket and I've seen what it does to grief. It wears the edges off. I decided I wasn't going to let it.

So I took the small notebook, the pencil one, and turned to the last page. At the top I wrote: *Names.* Under it, the name, the date, and one line about the person.

*Bohdan. Apologized to machines. Fifty-six of them.*

It felt like putting down a stone on a path so someone could find it later.

Since then, more. Pasha, whom I never met, in Halyna's words. A driver from the convoy who'd shared his thermos. A woman from the village who'd died in her house. A boy whose name I heard on the radio. A man from another unit with a very good laugh. There are fourteen now. I don't mention it. But last night Vira found it open on my bunk and read it without asking, which I'd normally mind. She stood in the lamplight for a long minute. Then she took the pencil very carefully and wrote one more name at the bottom, in her tidy hand. A friend of hers. A girl from Poltava. *She liked cake. She would've liked this idea.*

I said nothing. I nodded.

By morning more had appeared. Halyna's handwriting: a name, and a line, *My first patient, in the war. He lived. I like to think so.* Motor's block capitals, very small: his brother's. Zenko's elegant script: three names and *All of whom I taught.*

I looked at that last line. Zenko, forty-one, standing at the front of a class. Then a list.

It's a ledger now. Not mine. Ours. It sits on the shelf next to the cat mug, and people stop beside it on the way to the stove. Nobody opens it much. But they know it's there. They know the names are held.

I've decided to carry it with me. When I go home I'll take it. I'll read every name to Sonya when she's old enough, and tell her how each of them was kind.

There's one thing I did that I haven't told anyone. Under the last name, in pencil, very small, I wrote my own callsign, leaving a space for whoever comes next. Then I rubbed it out. Then I wrote it again and rubbed it out again, until the paper went thin.

It isn't a wish. It's arithmetic. Everyone in this book was a person who thought they had time. I rubbed it out because I'd like to keep the rest of the page clean for a while longer.

# Day 55 — Notes: Remembering

Names, on a page, a wall, a stone. Objects: a mug, a pocketknife, a hat. Stories, the best kind, told badly. Songs. A glass poured and not drunk. A candle.

I won't make anyone a symbol. They were people. They had bad moods and favorite socks.
