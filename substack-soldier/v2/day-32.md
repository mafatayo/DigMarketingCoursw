# Day 32 — Dispatch: The Workshop

I should explain the corner.

At the back of the dugout, behind a hanging blanket, there's a door laid across two crates, and on it lives Bohdan's workshop. A soldering iron with a burnt handle. A coil of thin wire. Tiny screwdrivers in a mug. Zip ties, tape in six colors, circuit boards with silver blobs of solder like tears. A magnifying glass on an old desk lamp. A jar of screws sorted by size, labeled in student handwriting: *M2, M2.5, "mystery."*

He sits there for hours with his tongue between his teeth, bent over a motor the size of a thumb, humming something that isn't a song.

I'd never really watched him work. Today I did. The twitchy apologetic kid disappears. His hands go still. His eyes sharpen. When a wire has to go somewhere, he puts it there with a patience that makes Halyna's hands look rough.

"What are you doing?"

"Fixing a motor. The bearing's worn. It sounds like a coffee grinder."

"Can you fix it?"

"I can make it limp for ten more flights." A flicker of pride. "Better than buying new. New takes a month."

A drone arrives bruised, with a bent arm, a cracked frame, a motor that won't turn, and he opens it like a surgeon. He talks to it. "Okay. Okay, good girl, I see, there's the problem." He patches a frame with plastic cut from a water bottle and a zip tie. Sometimes with a piece of a child's toy from a parcel. The little yellow wheel of a plastic truck is now, as far as I know, keeping a quadcopter level.

"Where do you learn it?"

"Videos. Forums. Breaking things. I broke eight before I understood one."

I asked if he ever felt strange about the work. He took a while.

"I think about it. But I think about the guys on the ground. If I look, they don't have to walk into it blind. That's all it is, sometimes." He turned the little motor in the lamplight. "The machine doesn't think. I do. I try to think well."

On the shelf above the bench there's a notebook with a green cover. I saw it open today by accident. Neat columns. A date, a number, a word. *Lost. Jammed. Lost. Shot. Lost.* He'd counted fifty-three. Each one with a small notation, sometimes a single word. *Sorry.*

"It's just for the donors," he said, seeing me look. "So I can tell them what happened."

"Do you tell them?"

"Not yet."

Later I heard the small motor start up again behind the blanket, not the whine of a flight, just a repaired thing spinning on a table, small and steady and alive.

# Day 32 — Notes: Parts

Duct tape, gray, black, and a roll of pink from a donor. It holds just as well and cheers everyone up. Zip ties. Old phone batteries; don't ask. A good magnifier. A cup of tea kept at arm's length, never closer; he ruined a board that way.

Parts from dead drones go back into live ones. Even a lost machine gives something back. I'd like it noted that this is the most humane sentence I've written in a month.
