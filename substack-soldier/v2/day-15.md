# Day 15 — Dispatch: The Middle Watch

**Bottom line:** On the middle watch I thought about my father, beehives, and what happens to a razor left on a shelf.

Midnight to two is the worst slot. You're too awake to sleep and too tired to be good at anything. I got it tonight, the first watch since Pasha, and nobody said anything about that, but Motor gave me his own thermos.

I went up with a blanket over my shoulders and the Colonel padding behind. A hard clean cold, the kind you can hear. More stars than I've seen since I was a boy, because nobody's turned on a light in this valley for months. Orion low in the south. The Plough tipped over like it had been dropped.

The first hour was fine. I did the checklist in my head, left, center, right, sound, smell, movement, left, center, right. A flare went up, far off, white and slow, and hung there, and everything under it looked like a photograph of itself. Then it went out and the dark was blacker.

The second hour was different, because the mind drifts and you can't stop it. I thought about my father, who died when I was twenty-six, who once drove four hours to bring me a forgotten jacket. I thought about the apartment, the creak of the third stair, how Sonya jumps from the bottom step on purpose to make it creak. About my last job before the call, a short documentary about a beekeeper in the Carpathians. I'd recorded his hives. Forty thousand bees, a living low hum, like a choir that never ends.

I wondered whether they were still there. Whether anybody was recording them. I decided it mattered more than almost anything, someone holding up a microphone to a small stubborn life.

Then a rustle on the left. My whole body snapped upright. The Colonel's ears went up. We both stared into the dark for I don't know how long.

A hedgehog, trundling along the wire. Absurdly slow. Utterly serious about its business.

I breathed out. It took a while.

I thought about Pasha then, as you do when you let your guard down. Specifically about the fact that someone had stood on this exact spot, on this exact crate, three weeks ago, and looked at the same stars. I'd been given his watch rota. Not stated, just inherited, like a coat. And I found I wasn't afraid of dying, exactly. I was afraid of becoming a razor on a shelf, a thing people leave at the same angle because they can't decide.

At two, Bohdan came up, hair wild, rubbing his eyes. "Anything?"

"A hedgehog."

"Dangerous?"

"Very."

He took the blanket, which I hadn't offered, and I went down into the warm. The room smelled of tea and old smoke and the slow breath of sleeping men. I lay there with the whole sky still in my head.

**Tactical read.** Night observation is slow and tiring, and fatigue degrades detection more than the lack of equipment does.

**OPSEC.** Positions, orientation of the line and distances are altered.

# Day 15 — Notes: To the Man at the Screen

**Bottom line:** To the man at the drone screen: look one second longer.

You have a mother, too. I know you do. I don't forgive you, and I'm not asking to be forgiven. I'm asking you to look at the screen for one second longer than your orders say and see what is on it.

It is a field. A road. A man with a mug. It was never an enemy. It was always just a person standing somewhere.
