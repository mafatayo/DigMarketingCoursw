# Day 27 — Dispatch: Mama

**Bottom line:** My mother broke her hip running to a basement during an alert, and I couldn't go to her.

The message came at four in the afternoon, which is a strange hour for bad news. I'd expected it at night. Instead it was a bright flat gray afternoon with Bohdan arguing about socks in the corner and the kettle just starting. My brother. Four words. *Call me. It's Mama.*

I went up the steps with shaking hands. The signal's bad in the dugout, you have to stand by the post at the far end, one bar flickering. I dialed. It rang six times.

"She fell," said Andriy, low and careful. "Last night. She broke her hip."

My mother is seventy-three. She lives alone on the fourth floor, in the flat I grew up in, no lift. She makes dumplings on Sundays. She grows basil in a yogurt pot on the windowsill. She won't be helped.

"How did she fall?"

A pause that told me everything.

"There was an alert," he said. "She was going down to the basement. She doesn't use the stairs fast. She says she's too old to run, so she goes down when the app buzzes instead of when the sound starts. She did that. But the first one was early. It was on the next street. She was on the second landing and the whole building shook and she lost her footing."

"Is she—"

"She's in the hospital. They operated this morning. It went well." Another pause. "She's awake. She's being herself. She told the surgeon his hands were cold."

I laughed. It came out as a sound I didn't know I could make. "That sounds like her."

"Danylo. She asked if you'd eaten."

I leaned my head on the post. The wind dragged across the field. A hundred meters off somebody was chopping wood, thock, thock.

What's hard is this: I'm four hundred kilometers from my mother's bed, in a hole, with no leave on offer. The reason she's in that bed is the same reason I'm here. She fell on a staircase, running from the thing I'm supposed to be stopping, and I stopped nothing, I was standing in the mud learning to light a stove. That's the logic of it. It doesn't help, but it's there.

"I can't come."

"I know. Nobody's asking you to."

"Tell her I'm eating. Tell her I'm eating a lot."

"I'll tell her."

"Tell her the dog likes me."

"What dog?"

"Just tell her."

When I came down, the dugout went quiet the way it does. Nobody asked. Halyna looked at my face and put a mug in my hands and sat down beside me, shoulder to shoulder, saying nothing. Motor, at the table, began humming his four bars, a bit louder than usual. Vira turned the radio down. Bohdan quietly moved his boots.

# Day 27 — Notes: The Stairs

**Bottom line:** The old people who fall on the stairs don't make the news.

Statistics that don't make the news: how many old people break a hip running to a shelter. My mother's one. There's a ward in her hospital with eleven of them. They didn't die. They fell.

Nobody counts the ones who fall.
