# Day 24 — Dispatch: Forty-One Books

**Bottom line:** A basement library keeps a gap on its shelf for the readers who don't return.

There's a library in a basement in the village and it has forty-one books. I counted twice. It's an addiction.

A retired schoolteacher called Mrs. Olena runs it. Tiny, sharp-eyed, silver hair pinned up in a style older than my mother. The basement of a cultural center, half collapsed above, stripped of furniture, is now her kingdom. Shelves made from planks and bricks. Two armchairs. A kettle. A heater when there's power. Over the door, a hand-lettered sign: SILENCE IS FOR THE LIBRARY. THE REST OF THE WORLD MAY SHOUT.

She keeps a ledger in careful cursive. *Borrowed by: Lys, Thursday. Due: when you can.*

I picked up a worn paperback, a collection of short stories, spine broken, a child's name written inside the cover. I read the whole first story in an armchair while the heater clicked, and for twenty minutes forgot where I was. I'd forgotten books do that. They take you out and stand you in a field outside yourself.

"The first story's the best," she said, over half-moon glasses. "Everyone says so."

"It's very good."

"It's about a man who walks home. A whole country, a hundred pages." She poured tea. "Soldiers like it. They don't say why."

I knew why.

She told me what they ask for. Funny books. Very old ones, anything before the twentieth century. And poetry, which surprised me. "Short," she said. "You can read a poem in a bad hour and put it in your pocket." She's given out forty-seven so far, she keeps a list. One young man took a poem and brought it back a week later with the page folded down and said, "I read it every night." She never asked which line.

Then, so quietly I almost missed it: "Some of them don't bring them back."

"The books?"

"Yes."

I didn't ask. I looked at the shelf of returned poems, thin pale spines in a row. There was one gap in the row. She'd left it.

"I keep the space," she said. "In case."

I borrowed the stories. She lent me a pencil too, so I could underline, a sin, she said, but a forgivable one. At the door she stopped me. "Come back and tell me how it ends."

"I will."

"Don't die before the last page."

I promised her I'd try.

# Day 24 — Notes: The Gap

**Bottom line:** Every kitchen here has a gap like Mrs. Olena's shelf.

Mrs. Olena keeps a gap on the poetry shelf for the ones who don't bring the books back. She's got four gaps now.

There are shelves like that in every kitchen in this country. A coat on a hook. A cup nobody uses. A chair nobody takes.
