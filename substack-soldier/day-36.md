*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 36 — Dispatch: Fear Has a Smell

Nobody tells you fear has a smell, and I'd like to correct the record.

It's metallic. Sharp, with a slight sourness, like a coin held under the tongue, or the inside of a cold pipe. It comes off the body. I noticed it first in the van, on the evacuation night, and thought it was the stretcher. Then it was in the dugout the next morning, hanging faintly in the air. Then I smelled it on my own sleeve, and understood that it was mine.

There's a theory, which Halyna holds, that the body knows. "Animals smell it," she says. "The Colonel can smell it. People can too, they just don't admit it."

The Colonel, in fact, was a better barometer than any of us. For two days after the night drive, he refused to leave Bohdan's side. Bohdan, who hadn't said much since, sat with his back against the wall, knees drawn up, staring at nothing, while the dog leaned against his shin.

I sat down next to him. I didn't say anything. After a while he said, "I thought I'd be fine."

"You were fine."

"I held the stretcher. I didn't drop it."

"You didn't drop it."

"But I couldn't feel my hands. After. I thought they'd been cut off. I thought, they're not mine."

I nodded. I'd felt the same. I told him so.

"How do you stop it?" he said.

"I don't think you stop it. I think you learn what it is." I rubbed my own palms together. "Zenko told me something once. He said fear isn't the opposite of courage. It's the *price.* Every brave thing costs a little fear, and you're just paying the bill."

"That's a very teacher thing to say."

"He's a teacher."

Bohdan almost smiled. We sat there in the faint, sour smell, while the Colonel breathed slowly between us, and I thought how strange it was that we could name so many fears (cold, dark, loneliness, the machine overhead) and none of them were quite this. This was the one the body made on its own, a small secret, a chemical.

That night I couldn't sleep, and neither could he. At two a.m. he whispered from the bunk below mine, "Lys? Are you scared right now?"

"Yes."

"Good," he said. "Me too. I just wanted to check it wasn't only me."

# Day 36 — Notes: Naming Fear

A short, honest inventory.

**The fears I've met here:**
- The fear of dying. The one you'd expect. Weirdly, it's not the largest.
- The fear of being the reason someone else does.
- The fear of freezing, of doing nothing when it counts.
- The fear of not being believed, afterward.
- The fear of going home changed.
- The fear of a call at the wrong hour.
- The fear that I'm already forgetting my daughter's voice.

**What helps (a few things that have worked):**
1. Saying it, to one person, in daylight.
2. A small task. Anything with hands.
3. Breathing slow, four counts in, six out.
4. Remembering that everyone else near you is scared too.
5. Letting the body shake. It will pass faster.

**What doesn't help:** Calling yourself a coward. You're not. A coward wouldn't have shown up.
