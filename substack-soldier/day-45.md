*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 45 — Dispatch: Radio Silence

We were ordered off our phones for three days, and I learned how much of my mind lived inside one.

I won't give the reason, because I don't know all of it and because it isn't mine to share. The order came down quietly: no calls, no messages, no photos, no signal. Phones in a bag, a bag in a box, a box under a bunk. Motor collected them one by one, with Halyna watching, and each man handed his over like a child surrendering a toy.

Mine felt heavier than it should. It was warm from my pocket. For a second I almost asked to keep it, just to hold.

The first day was withdrawal, plain and absurd. My thumb kept twitching toward a pocket that wasn't there. At every pause, a lull at breakfast, a stretch between jobs, my hand slid to my hip and found nothing. Bohdan, who lives in his phone, looked like a man who'd lost a limb. By noon he had started cleaning his rifle for the fourth time.

By the second day, something shifted. The twitching faded. Without the small buzzing weather of messages and news and notifications, the world got bigger and slower. I noticed things. The color of the sky at five o'clock, a pale peach going to green. The way Zenko's laugh starts in his shoulders. The fact that Yaroslav hums when he thinks nobody's listening, and what he hums is the same four bars as Motor.

We talked. A great deal. We played cards for hours. We told stories we hadn't told. Vira taught Yaroslav a drinking song with no drink. Halyna read aloud from the dragon book again, and the grown men gathered like kindergartners.

On the third night, a remarkable thing happened: nobody wanted their phones back. Not exactly. We wanted the *people* in them. But the hum had gone out of us, and we noticed, without saying, that we were calmer.

Then the order lifted. Motor opened the box. Phones came out, one by one, and lit up like fireflies. A wave of buzzing, pinging, vibrating. Thirty-seven messages for me. Eleven from Katya. One from Sonya, a voice note, a small bright voice saying, "Papa, I lost a tooth."

I went outside and played it nine times.

# Day 45 — Notes: Communication Without Phones

How we managed, in a few small ways.

- **Runners.** A man with a note, on foot. Old as the hills.
- **Written messages.** A pencil, a scrap. Don't underestimate it.
- **Hand signals.** A short vocabulary. Thumb up, flat palm, finger to lips.
- **Shared plans.** Everyone knows the plan, so nobody needs to ask.
- **Face to face.** Wonderful, once you remember how.
- **Waiting.** Which, as I've said, is half the job.

**What I discovered:** I'd been using my phone not to communicate but to *avoid* the people in front of me. It's easy to look down. It's harder to look up.

**What I'd suggest:** Try a day. See what the quiet says.
