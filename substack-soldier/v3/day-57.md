# Day 57 — Boxes: Donations Need Triage

*Notes on how supply meets mismatch.*

**Bottom line:** A donation truck brought the right batteries, and Yaroslav realized they were Bohdan's.

**OPSEC & Context:** *Fiction.* The narrator is a mobilized sound engineer serving in a small drone-support section. Equipment counts, stock levels and supply routes are withheld.

## The Reality on the Ground

A truck of donations arrived today and Motor opened it the way a man opens a letter from the tax office.

"Check the labels. I want to know what every box is before we touch it."

It's a sensible policy. Not every donation helps. Over the weeks we've received some astonishing things. A crate of fine porcelain teacups. Seventeen pairs of ice skates. A box of ties. A large framed painting of a lake with a note: *For morale.* A small kind bewildering sack of confetti.

So we approached this load warily. Vira read the labels out loud. "Batteries... bandages... wool socks... thermal underwear..."

"Oh, thank God," said Yaroslav.

"...a bag of oranges... duct tape, forty rolls..."

"Forty!"

"...children's letters... a harmonica..."

We went through it box by box, a long delighted afternoon. By dusk the dugout looked like a general store. Halyna was stacking bandages like a bank robber. Yaroslav sat on a crate with a box of electronics, the face of a boy at the end of a very good holiday, and then, as I watched, the face changed. He'd opened a case of small batteries, the kind Bohdan used. He stood very still holding one in his palm.

"They're the right ones," he said. "He kept asking for these."

Zenko took the case from him gently and set it aside. "We'll keep them," he said. "For when someone else comes."

At the bottom of one crate I found a note from the people who'd packed it. Eight volunteers in a hall in a city I'll never see, at long tables, with a handwritten inventory. They'd sorted the socks by size. They'd put a tangerine in each box. A woman had drawn a small smiling sun on the lid in marker and signed it *Nadia.*

I thought about her, bending over a table, thinking of a stranger who would wear these. She'd never know. She'd never see Motor, a large man, hold a pair of wool socks to his face and close his eyes.

You can't see the whole chain. You see the boxes and the dust on them and the writing. Behind each one is a kitchen table, a woman, a Tuesday evening spent folding clothes for strangers.

"We should write back," said Halyna.

"To who?"

"All of them."

We didn't, of course. But I wrote one note and taped it to the lid of the next box going back down the line. *To whoever packed this: you were seen.*

## Tactical Observations

- Donations need triage and screening, because mismatched items consume transport and storage.
