# Day 84 — The Tin: Writing to the Future

*Notes on rituals that anchor meaning.*

**Bottom line:** We wrote letters to the children who'll live after this and sealed them in a tin.

**OPSEC & Context:** *Fiction.* The narrator is a mobilized sound engineer serving in a small drone-support section. Anything that could identify a place, a time or a person's unit has been changed.

## The Reality on the Ground

Zenko had an idea, and for once it wasn't about Plutarch.

"We should write to the children," he said. "Not our own. The ones who'll come after. The ones who'll go to school in the buildings we've drawn on the wall."

"What would we say?" said Vira.

"The truth. Gently."

It took a week. Each of us, one by one, in the quiet hours, took a sheet and a pencil and tried. Yaroslav wrote an entire page and burned it in the stove and wrote another. Halyna produced a short list, crossed it out, and wrote one sentence.

We put them in a biscuit tin with a dented lid. Zenko will keep it. "I'll open it," he said, "when we win. In a classroom."

*From Halyna:* "Wash your hands. Be kind to nurses. Drink water. Everything else is detail."

*From Vira:* "Learn to listen. It's the most useful thing I know. And laugh. Loudly. Someone will thank you."

*From Yaroslav:* "Look after the animals. They didn't ask for any of this."

*From Motor, in blocky capitals:* "BE ON TIME. HELP WITH THE BUCKETS. SING."

*From Zenko, in a long careful beautiful hand:* "You will be told that history is a list of dates. It is not. History is a very large number of tired people doing small decent things, mostly unobserved. You are the next ones. Do likewise."

We'd all stopped. There was a space on the table where a letter should have been. Vira took a sheet and wrote on it, in her tidy hand, and I read it over her shoulder.

*From Bohdan (as best we can):* "Make things. Fix things. Don't be afraid of a small broken machine. Be kind to it. Be kinder to the people who built it. And if you break one, say sorry anyway."

"He didn't write that," said Yaroslav.

"No," said Vira. "He'd have written it if we'd asked." She folded it. "Somebody has to."

And mine:

*Dear child,*

*I don't know your name or your face or whether you like sunflower seeds. But I know that you're reading this in a room with a window, and that there is no siren.*

*I'd like to tell you what the world sounded like in the year I wrote this. It sounded like a kettle and a stove and a man humming four bars of a song about a bus. It sounded like a carol on a radio in the dark, sung by a hundred strangers. It sounded like a lark, going up.*

*Listen. There's always something under the first sound. Listen under it.*

*Be gentle. It's very easy. It's also very rare.*

*With love from the past,*

*Lys.*

We sealed the tin with a strip of pink duct tape. Zenko put it on the highest shelf beside the cat mug.
