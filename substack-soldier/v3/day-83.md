# Day 83 — Tuesdays: Talking About After

*Notes on coping and retention.*

**Bottom line:** We each described our perfect Tuesday, and we left a chair for Bohdan.

**OPSEC & Context:** *Fiction.* The narrator is a mobilized sound engineer serving in a small drone-support section. Anything that could identify a place, a time or a person's unit has been changed.

## The Reality on the Ground

Tonight we did something we'd been avoiding. We described, in detail, our perfect ordinary day.

It was Vira's idea. A damp quiet evening, the stove glowing, rain on the roof like a thousand small fingers. She was darning a sock by the lamp with her tongue between her teeth. "Let's do a game. Tell me a day. Not a great day. A Tuesday. The best, plainest Tuesday you can imagine, when it's over."

"That's a dangerous game," said Halyna.

"Why?"

"Because we'll want it."

"We want it already. Go."

Zenko first. "I wake at eight. No alarm. A window open, a smell of coffee, a dull wireless. I read for an hour in a chair that fits. I walk to the market and buy an enormous melon. I come home and eat it, entirely, alone, with a small spoon."

"That's it?" said Yaroslav.

"That is *everything.*"

Yaroslav: "I wake and the cows are making noise. I do the milking. It's cold and my hands are warm from the cow. I eat bread and cream. My mother's yelling about something, in a nice way."

Vira: "I wake up late. My mother's cooking. I go to the bakery with my cousin, we eat cake on a bench, talk nonsense for three hours, and a man with a guitar plays something stupid."

Halyna: "I sleep until the sun hits my face. I water the garden. I pick a tomato and eat it standing up with salt. A neighbor drops by with plums. I'm on the porch until dusk with a book and nobody needs me."

"Nobody needs you?" said Vira.

"For one day. Just one."

Then everyone turned to Motor, who'd been silent, whittling. He looked up. For a moment I thought he'd refuse. Then he said in his low gravel voice, "I get home at six. The bus is late as usual. She's at the stop with the thermos. She has a scarf on. She's looking down the road, and I come round the corner, and she sees me."

He stopped. Nobody breathed.

"That's the day. Everything else I can improvise."

I didn't say mine aloud. But I thought it in the silence that followed. A kitchen. A kettle. Two hands, one small, one large. A stair that creaks. A third, softer hand laid on top.

The rain went on, and the lamp, and the darning needle, and the Colonel sighed in his sleep. There was one more chair at the crate than anyone was using, and we'd left it where it was.

"We should say Bohdan's," said Yaroslav.

Then Vira said, in a voice I'd never heard her use, "He'd fix something. A radio, an old one. Nobody in a hurry. It works. Everyone's pleased."

"That's it," said Halyna.

"That's exactly it."

For a long time nobody wanted to be the first to speak.

**Bottom line:** Justice looks like a Tuesday.

We all described the same day. A kitchen, a market, a bus stop, a garden. Nobody asked for a victory parade. Nobody said "justice."

Justice is the Tuesday. That's what it looks like when you get it.
