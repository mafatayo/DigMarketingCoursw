# Day 88 — Dispatch: To Someone Who's Never Been

You've asked me, in different ways, for three months, what it's *really* like. Here's my best attempt, with the understanding that it'll fail.

Imagine the loudest day of your life, a day when something enormous happened. Now imagine it's over. And then imagine a hundred days of waiting for it to happen again. Most of it's that. A long wait, in the cold, with people you've come to love, doing small jobs.

Imagine being tired, not just at night, but in a thick layer that never quite lifts. Imagine being so used to it that when you finally sleep eleven hours, you wake up weeping.

Imagine a kettle as the center of your whole moral world.

Imagine knowing precisely how far a kind word can carry. How a stranger's letter can hold you up for a week.

Imagine finding out that you can laugh in the worst possible place, and that nobody thinks the less of you.

Imagine finding that your grandmother was right about dill.

Imagine a dog who leans.

Imagine that every night, before sleeping, you say a short list of names, and every morning, you say them again.

Imagine that a woman you've never met gets in a car and drives for seven hours to put a bowl of soup in your hands, and she won't even give you her name.

Imagine being scared and doing the next thing.

Imagine missing someone so much it's a physical object, a weight, a stone in your pocket you can't put down, and you also wouldn't put it down if you could.

Imagine, finally, that you're good at something you never expected to be good at. And you wish with your whole heart you'd never had to learn it.

That's the closest I can come. If you're someone who's never been, who watches from a distance, I want you to know: it isn't a failure of yours. Don't feel guilty. Don't feel you owe a performance of suffering. Just stay human. Stay curious. Stay kind. Be a person who, if it ever came to it, would carry the water.

I think that's what the rest of us are asking for. That's all.

# Day 88 — Notes: Questions Readers Asked

These are the questions I get most. Short answers.

**"Are you afraid?"** Yes. Every day. It's okay.

**"Do you hate them?"** I try not to think about hate. It's heavy. I think about the person next to me.

**"What do you miss most?"** Doors. Quiet. My daughter's feet.

**"What do you eat?"** Buckwheat. Pickles. Whatever somebody's grandmother sent.

**"How can I help?"** Be steady. Give a little, regularly. Don't look away.

**"Will you come back?"** I'm working on it. It's the only plan I have.
