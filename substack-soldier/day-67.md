*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 67 — Dispatch: Leave, Day Three

On the third day I went to the bakery.

It's the one I'd walked past that first morning, the lights just coming on, the smell following me for two blocks. I'd thought about it for months. A silly thing to hold onto. But it had become, in my mind, a sort of lighthouse. Some mornings, standing watch, I'd conjure the smell and let it warm me.

I walked there slowly, in a clean coat Katya had pressed, past the same iron gate, the same dented bin. The street looked smaller. It's a thing people say, but it's true. Everything had shrunk, by some trick of scale, and the people on it seemed oddly weightless, hurrying, scrolling, chatting in twos.

At the door I stopped. Through the glass I saw the baker, a heavy woman in a white apron, arranging rolls on a tray. She looked up, saw me, and froze.

She came out from behind the counter, wiping her hands on her apron. "You're the sound man," she said.

"I'm sorry?"

"The one from the third floor of number twelve. Katya's husband. You used to buy the poppy-seed rolls on Fridays."

"Yes."

She looked me up and down. Slowly. The coat, the scar of tiredness on my face, the new thinness, the way I stood. Her expression changed. A softening, a collapse, as if something she'd been holding up for a long time had finally been allowed to rest.

"Sit," she said. "Sit. I'm making you a roll."

"I don't need to..."

"Sit."

I sat at the little table by the window. She brought me a poppy-seed roll, hot, split, buttered. And a coffee. And a second roll, because the first was gone before she'd turned. I ate with my head down. She stood by, hands folded, watching me with an expression I knew: the look of the woman with the borshch, of Lidiya, of every grandmother in the world.

"My son's out there," she said, quietly. "Not far from where you've been, I think. He's a driver." She took a breath. "I don't know where. I don't ask. When I see one of you, I feed them. I think maybe somewhere, someone's feeding him."

"I'm sure of it."

"Is it hard?"

I looked at the roll. The poppy seeds on my fingers. "Yes," I said. "And it's also... full. Of good people."

She nodded, and her eyes shone. She wouldn't take my money. When I left she pressed a paper bag into my hand, six rolls, still warm.

I walked home slowly, the bag against my chest, the smell rising. And this time, I understood the smell hadn't been a lighthouse. It had been a promise. That there would be a morning, a door, a woman who gave you a roll for no reason at all.

# Day 67 — Notes: Being Seen

What it's like, on leave, among people who haven't been.

- **They look at me longer.** Quiet, searching looks.
- **They stand up on the tram.** I've asked them not to.
- **They say "thank you."** I never know what to answer. "You're welcome" feels wrong.
- **They ask nothing.** Or too much.
- **They give me rolls.** Always rolls.

**What I'm learning:** Being seen isn't the same as being understood. But it's something. A look can be a kind of warmth.

**What I do now:** I just say "Thank you for the roll."
