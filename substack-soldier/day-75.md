*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 75 — Dispatch: Spring Rumors

It begins with a smell.

I was outside at dawn, as usual, with the kettle, and the air had changed. Not warmer, exactly. Softer. There was something in it, a damp richness, like turned earth, like the first moment you open a door on a garden. I stood still with my face lifted, breathing, until the kettle shrieked.

"What is it?" said Halyna, coming up behind me, wrapped in her enormous coat.

"Spring," I said. "I think."

"Don't say that."

"Why?"

"It jinxes it."

But we both smelled it. Under the cold, the ghost of something green.

By afternoon, there was dripping. A steady plink from the eaves of the dugout, each drop catching the pale sun. The snow in the fields had gone gray and sagging, with dark patches showing through, like the skin of an old animal shedding its coat. A single crow stood in the middle of the nearest patch, looking gravely at the mud, as if reading a contract.

The rumors, I should tell you, begin at the same time. Every thaw, as I'm learning, is accompanied by an upsurge of whispering. This year it's about something new in the plans, a shift, an event, a push. Nobody knows what. Everybody has a view. Bohdan thinks it's coming in a week. Vira says a month. Zenko says history suggests that spring and war have always been unpleasant bedfellows, and then apologizes for the phrase.

The truth is we don't know. We never do. We only feel the air change.

There's a fear in the thaw, and I'd like to name it. It isn't the fear of fighting. It's the fear of *waiting through* another summer. Winter is a kind of pause, a shared frozen breath. Spring means movement, new choices, new danger. The very thing that makes the world beautiful makes it harder to survive.

And yet. I stood on the sandbags tonight and watched the light go. The sky was the pale green of a young leaf. Somewhere a bird, the first I'd heard in weeks, tried out a phrase, got it wrong, tried again.

I felt it rise in me, treacherous and sweet. Hope. That unreliable guest. I let it in. I let it sit at my table, for the length of a single song.

Then Motor, from the steps, called, "Lys. Water."

And I went.

# Day 75 — Notes: Hope and Its Risks

A cautious guide.

**Hope is dangerous because:**
- It can make you careless.
- It can make you bitter, if it's dashed.
- It can make you forget the cold.

**Hope is necessary because:**
- Without it, nobody gets up.
- It makes a very long night bearable.
- It's the thing you give each other.

**How I carry it:** Lightly. Like an egg. Not clutching, not dropping.

**What helps:** Small hopes. Tomorrow's tea. Next week's mail. The first green shoot by the sandbags.

What small hope are you holding right now?
