*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 19 — Dispatch: The Rotation Rumor

It began, as these things do, with a man who knew a man.

Bohdan heard it from a driver, who'd heard it from a cook, who'd heard it from "someone in the staff." The rumor: our section would be rotated out in a week. Five days of rest at a rear village. Real beds. Real showers. A roof that did not leak into pots.

By dinner everyone had heard it. By evening everyone believed it. By midnight everyone had begun, quietly, to plan. I watched it happen, and it was fascinating and a little sad. Men who hadn't smiled in a week were describing, in great detail, what they'd do on day one. Bohdan would sleep for fourteen hours. Halyna would have a bath so long that the water would go cold twice. Zenko wanted a library. Vira wanted a bakery and a man with a guitar. Motor said nothing, but I caught him folding and refolding a clean shirt.

Then Motor, near midnight, called us together and said quietly, "Listen. I don't know anything. Nobody has told me anything. If it's true, good. If it's not, don't let me see you sulk."

It was a hard thing to say. He saw the faces. He said it anyway.

This is the cruelest trick of the front, I think. Not the shelling. The hope. Hope is a loose tooth. You can't stop touching it. And a rumor of rest does something strange to the body: suddenly you notice every ache. The cold gets colder. The mud gets muddier. You've been living with it all along, but now you know there might be a different world nearby, and the difference hurts.

Day two of the rumor, nothing. Day three, nothing. By day four the mood sank. Someone muttered that it was all talk. Vira snapped at the radio. Bohdan didn't talk much. On day five, a truck came, bringing ammunition and more water, and a young officer who looked at our hopeful faces and said, "No rotation. Maybe next month."

Nobody said anything. We just nodded. Someone put the kettle on.

I lay awake that night and thought about it. We hadn't really lost anything. It hadn't been ours. But we'd grieved it, the way you grieve a house you walked through once and almost bought.

In the morning Motor handed out chores like always. "Buckets," he said. "Boots. Firewood." Bohdan, red-eyed, looked up and half-smiled. We went to work. Rest, I thought, isn't a place. It's whatever you can steal from the day.

# Day 19 — Notes: Waiting Is Work

Most of this war, for most of us, is waiting. Nobody warned me it would be this exhausting.

**Types of waiting I've done:**
- Waiting for an order.
- Waiting for the truck.
- Waiting for a call to connect.
- Waiting for a rumor to be true.
- Waiting for the rain to stop.
- Waiting for someone to come back.
- Waiting for nothing at all.

**What I've learned:**
1. Waiting uses energy. Don't pretend it's rest.
2. Give it a job. Clean something. Mend something. Write a letter.
3. Don't invest in rumors. Treat them as weather reports from another country.
4. Be gentle with the person next to you. They're waiting, too.
5. Eat when you can. Sleep when you can. That's the whole discipline.

What are you waiting for right now?
