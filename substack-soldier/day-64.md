# Day 64 — Dispatch: Video Call Home

I saw my daughter's face on Saturday, for the first time in months, and I forgot every word I'd rehearsed.

The signal had been good for two days, a rare gift, and Motor, who understands such things, said quietly, "Lys, take your phone out. Go up by the post. Call them. Now."

So I did. I stood in the snow in my enormous coat, with my hat pulled down, holding the phone at arm's length, and heard it ring. Once, twice. A flicker. And then, suddenly, there they were: a bright small window of kitchen light, a tilted ceiling, a very big blurry eye.

"Papa!" said Sonya, and the phone swung wildly. "Papa, Papa, it's *Papa!*"

I'd practiced a speech. I'd planned something calm and fatherly. Instead I just laughed, an idiot's grin spreading over my whole face, tears running down into my collar, and said, "Hello, my sunshine. Hello. Hello."

She'd grown. I'd known she would. But I'd been carrying in my mind a smaller person, the one who'd waved from the doorway, and this one was taller, more serious, with a new gap in her teeth and a fringe someone had cut with kitchen scissors. She held up a toy rabbit. "This is Marmalade. He's been waiting."

"Hello, Marmalade."

"He says you look thin."

"He's right."

Behind her, Katya appeared, hovering, in a gray sweater, her hair longer. She didn't say anything. She just stood there with her hand pressed against her mouth and her eyes enormous. When Sonya finally let her have the phone, she held it very close to her face, so I saw only a wide brown eye and a trembling lip.

"You look tired," she said at last.

"I am."

"You look *wonderful.*"

"I look like a scarecrow."

"A scarecrow I'd know anywhere."

We talked for twenty-three minutes. About nothing. The tap, the cat, Sonya's school, a bad tooth, a new neighbor with a parrot. I kept turning my head away from the phone so she wouldn't see me crying, and then turning it back, because I couldn't bear to lose her face.

When the signal began to break, Sonya pressed her nose against the screen. "Papa. Are you coming home?"

I looked at her. At that small, serious, hopeful face.

"When," I said. "When, Sonya."

The line cut. For a long moment I stood very still in the snow with my phone in my mittened hand and the white fields all around me. Behind me, softly, I heard Motor humming. He'd been standing a respectful twenty paces away the whole time.

# Day 64 — Notes: The Strange Intimacy of Screens

I'm ambivalent, honestly.

**What it gives:**
- A face. A voice. A room behind them.
- A child's grin, in real time.
- The sense that you aren't missing everything.

**What it takes:**
- The whole call ends, and you're back in the snow, more alone than before.
- You see how much they've changed.
- You can't hold a screen.
- The goodbye is brutal. A button, and gone.

**What I do:**
1. Look at the face, not at myself.
2. Don't plan too much. Let it wander.
3. Say one true thing before the end.
4. Afterwards, walk. Don't go straight in.
5. Let someone put tea in your hand.

If you have someone far away, call them. Don't text. Call.
