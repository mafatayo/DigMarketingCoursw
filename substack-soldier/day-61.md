# Day 61 — Dispatch: Winter Quiet

*A note on the numbering: from here, the dispatches come further apart. The nights are long and the signal is poor, so I write when I can. I've kept the count anyway. It helps me know where I am.*

The snow came overnight and took the sound out of the world.

I woke to a silence so deep I thought I'd gone deaf. I lay there with my eyes open, listening for the stove, for Motor's snore, for the Colonel's thick breathing. Everything was there, but muffled, as if wrapped in felt. When I climbed the steps and pushed the door, a cold white light fell in, and the whole field lay under a smooth blanket, unmarked, shining, without a single footprint.

I stood there a long time. I'm told that snow absorbs sound, that the little hollows in each flake swallow it. I'd known this once as a technical fact, in my old life, when I was setting up microphones. Now I felt it in my chest. The war seemed to have been taken somewhere far away and put to sleep.

It doesn't last, of course. By midday, tracks. Vehicle ruts. A black scar where the stove ash had been thrown. Someone's boots wandering in a drunk line to the latrine. But for the first hour, the field was a clean page.

Winter, here, slows everything. The days are short. Darkness comes at four. The cold takes the edge off the thinking part of the brain, and we move like old men, saving every motion. Fires matter more than rifles, almost. We spend half the day gathering, chopping, stacking wood, and we guard it like gold.

And yet it has its own kind of peace. Long nights around the stove. Cards, stories, soup. Vira has taken up knitting, badly, and produces a long gray rectangle that she calls "a scarf for someone with a very long neck." Zenko reads aloud from a thick book, with the Colonel's head on his foot. Bohdan has begun to teach Yaroslav the guitar.

I took a photograph of the snow on my phone, for Sonya. I haven't sent it. I don't know how to explain it. A child in a flat with a radiator wouldn't understand what it means to have the whole world turn white and still.

At dusk I stood on the sandbags again. The sky was a rare pale gold, and the snow took the color, and everything glowed. The cold bit my face, but I didn't go in. I thought: this is what peace looks like, in a language I'd almost forgotten.

Then somewhere far off, a thud. And I went in for tea.

# Day 61 — Notes: Time Moves Differently

I've been trying to describe it.

**In the summer, time is a river.** It runs fast. Things happen.

**In winter, time is a lake.** It sits. Sometimes it freezes over.

**At the front, time is two things at once:** painfully slow (a night watch) and shockingly quick (two months gone).

**What I notice:**
- Days have no names.
- Hours have faces: the tea hour, the mail hour, the dark hour.
- A week feels like a day. A day feels like a week.
- Birthdays surprise me.

**What I do about it:** I keep the notebook. A line a day. Otherwise it all smears.

How do you keep track of time when it won't behave?
