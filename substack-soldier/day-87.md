# Day 87 — Dispatch: What I Believe Now

I've been trying to put together a short statement of what's left, after eighty-seven days. Not a manifesto. More like the list at the bottom of a rucksack, the stuff that's still there when everything else has been unpacked.

**I believe in small decency.** I used to think it was a lesser thing, a pleasant extra. I now think it's the structure the world hangs on. A cup of tea put in the right hand at the right moment holds up more than most treaties.

**I believe that fear and courage live in the same house.** They share a bed. They always have. A man is brave when he's afraid and does the next thing anyway. Anyone who tells you otherwise has never met either.

**I believe that people are better than the news makes them look.** And worse than they believe themselves to be, on bad days. Both. The trick is to hold both.

**I believe in jokes.** Seriously. A good joke at the right time is an act of love.

**I believe in listening.** It's the thing I'm best at, and the thing the world needs most. Not the loud kind. The kind where you put your own thoughts down and let the other person's fill the room.

**I believe we're not alone.** I've seen too many strangers do too many kind things to think anything else. A box. A letter. A pot of borshch, driven seven hours.

**I believe grief is the shadow of love.** It's made of the same stuff. You can't have the one without the other. I'm learning to carry mine like a coat.

**I believe in kitchens.** In ordinary rooms. In the right to a quiet Tuesday. If I've learned anything, it's that those are the whole prize.

**I believe that I've changed, and that it isn't over.** I don't know who I'll be at home. I'd like to be gentler, and braver, and a better listener. A better husband. A better father. A man who notices the bread.

I don't believe all of this every day. Some days I believe none of it. Some days I just believe in socks. But I've written it here, in pencil, so I can read it on the bad days and find out who I was when I was trying.

Motor read over my shoulder this morning, which he does, silently, like a very large owl. "Add one," he said.

"What?"

"*I believe the kettle goes to the line.*"

"That's Vira's position."

"I know," said Motor. "I'm trying to start a peace."

# Day 87 — Notes: Values Tested

What I thought I believed, before; what I think now.

| Before | Now |
|---|---|
| Courage is loud. | Courage is quiet. |
| Strength is hard. | Strength is soft, and persistent. |
| Be self-reliant. | Be generous, and accept help. |
| Fear is shameful. | Fear is information. |
| Home is a place. | Home is people. |

I'd like to hear yours. Not the grand ones. The ones that survived.
