# Day 69 — Dispatch: The Friend Who Didn't Understand

I met my old friend Pavlo for coffee on the fifth day of leave, and it was the loneliest forty minutes of my trip.

He's an old friend, the real kind. We went to school together. He was my best man. He's a good, funny, kind person with a wide laugh and a habit of finishing my sentences wrong. A software developer, he works from home, and he's remained safe, as people should. I'm glad. I want him safe.

We sat in a café with warm yellow lights and good coffee. He looked well. Rested. He'd put on a bit of weight and had a new haircut. For five minutes we talked about nothing, football, his girlfriend, his new flat, and it was lovely. A dip into a warm bath.

Then he asked.

"So," he said, leaning forward. "What's it *like?*"

And I didn't know what to say.

I opened my mouth. I thought of Halyna's hands, the smell of fear, the name at the table. I thought of the snow and the carol and the cat mug. I thought, how do you hand a person something that heavy, over a cappuccino, between a croissant and a bill?

"It's hard," I said.

"I can imagine."

"I don't think you can."

It came out sharper than I'd meant. He blinked. I saw the small hurt cross his face, quickly, like a cloud over a field. "Sorry," he said. "I just want to understand."

"I know. I know you do."

"Is there anything I can do?"

"Not really."

A silence. The café rattled around us with spoons and laughter. Pavlo stirred his coffee, though he'd drunk it already.

"I feel guilty," he said, quietly. "Sometimes. That I'm here. That you're there."

"Don't."

"I do."

"Pavlo." I put my hand over his. "You're the reason it's worth it. Understand? You, with your stupid haircut and your girlfriend and your flat. That's what we're keeping. If you were out there too, I'd be miserable."

He laughed, thickly. "My haircut's *not* stupid."

"It's a bit stupid."

We sat there. I realized that the gap between us wasn't anger or blame. It was just distance, the long cold gap between two kinds of life. He couldn't cross it, and I couldn't carry him over, and that was no one's fault.

As I left, he hugged me hard. "Come back," he said. "Come back so I can be a better listener."

"You're a good listener."

"I'll *practice,*" he said.

# Day 69 — Notes: Explaining the Unexplainable

Some things I've learned about trying.

1. **You don't have to explain it all.** A little is enough.
2. **Pick one small true thing.** "The tea tastes better there."
3. **Let them ask the wrong questions.** It's love, clumsily.
4. **Don't punish them for being safe.** That's what we want.
5. **Let silence do some of the work.**
6. **Find the one who listens.** There's always one.
7. **Write it down.** Even if you never show anyone.
