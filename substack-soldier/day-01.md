*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 1 — Dispatch: The Phone Call at 5 a.m.

The phone does not ring at 5 a.m. It buzzes against the nightstand like a trapped insect, and you know before you look.

I am a sound engineer. I spend my days making sure nobody hears the hum in the walls. So I noticed the hum first: the refrigerator, the street, my wife breathing. I thought, *this is the last time I will hear this exact room.* Then I answered.

The voice on the line was polite. Polite is worse. It gave me a time, an address, and a list of things to bring, as if I were being invited to a conference.

I made tea. I do not know why. My hands needed a job. Katya came into the kitchen in her sweater and did not ask. She just took the second cup down from the shelf and set it beside mine. We drank without talking. The kettle ticked as it cooled.

Our daughter was asleep. I stood in her doorway longer than I should have. She sleeps with one foot outside the blanket, always has. I did not wake her. I tell myself that was kindness. It may have been cowardice. Both can be true.

At the door Katya fixed my collar, which did not need fixing. "Call when you can," she said. "Eat something." That was all. Twelve years of marriage and the most important sentence was about eating, and it was exactly right.

I walked to the bus stop past the bakery that was just turning its lights on. The smell of bread followed me for two blocks. I will carry that smell a long time.

I want to be honest with you from the first page: I am not brave. I am a man with a bag, a list, and a kettle's worth of courage borrowed from my wife. If you came here for a hero, I will disappoint you. If you came to find out what an ordinary person does on the first morning of an extraordinary life, sit down. I will tell you everything I'm allowed to.

Tomorrow: the first night.

# Day 1 — Notes: What I Packed, What I Wish I Hadn't

**Packed and glad:**
1. Four pairs of wool socks. Nothing else comes close.
2. A small notebook and two pencils. Pens freeze; pencils don't.
3. A photo of Katya and our daughter, printed. Phones die.
4. Foot powder, blister plasters, a spare lace.
5. A headlamp with a red mode.

**Packed and regretted:**
1. A hardcover book. Heavy, and I read it in one night.
2. Three T-shirts of cotton. Cotton stays wet and keeps the cold.
3. The good razor. There is no mirror.
4. My pride. It went in the bottom of the bag and was the first thing I took out.

**The lesson:** Pack for the third day, not the first. The first day you are running on adrenaline. The third day your feet decide who you are.

**Question for you:** What would you put in a bag if you had one hour? Tell me in the comments. I read them all, usually at night, when there is nothing else to read.
