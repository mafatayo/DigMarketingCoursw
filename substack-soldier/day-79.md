# Day 79 — Dispatch: Mentoring

Yaroslav asked me to teach him to listen.

It was a Sunday evening, rainy, with a long gray light, and the two of us were on the steps, sharing a bench and a bag of sunflower seeds. He'd been quiet most of the day. I'd learned to read it, the particular dip of the shoulders that meant he was working up to something. I waited, cracking seeds, spitting the husks into the mud.

"Lys," he said at last. "You can tell what's coming. Before the rest of us. How?"

"It's the job."

"No. It's not only that. Bohdan can't do it. Vira can, a bit. But you do it like... like a trick."

"It isn't a trick." I thought about it. "It's patience. And a lot of mistakes."

"Can you teach it?"

I'd never been asked. I'd never thought of it as a thing that could be taught. I'd learned it the long way, in a studio, headphones on, listening to a thousand hours of nothing until I could hear the hum in a wall. But I looked at his face, young and serious and a little scared, and I said, "Close your eyes."

"Now?"

"Yes. Close them. And tell me everything you hear."

He did. He sat on the step with his eyes shut and his hands loose on his knees. For a minute he said nothing. Then, slowly: "Rain. Dripping. Someone coughing, far off. The stove. A truck, a long way away. A... bird."

"What kind?"

"I don't know."

"Listen again."

"A small one. High. It goes up at the end."

"That's a lark. It's the third one. The other two are nearer. Do you hear?"

He frowned. Then his eyes opened. "I do. I really do. There's three."

"That's all it is," I said. "You listen for what's under the first layer. Then under the next. Eventually you stop being surprised."

We did it every evening after that. Ten minutes, eyes closed, naming what we heard. He got better quickly, faster than I had. After a week, he caught a thing I'd missed, a faint, regular tapping that turned out to be a loose bit of metal on a distant roof. "How did you hear that?" I said, genuinely amazed.

"I just listened under," he said, and grinned.

I think this is what teaching is. Not handing over a fact. Handing over a way of paying attention. I'd never felt it before, the quiet, almost tender pride of seeing someone do what you do, a little better.

Zenko, who'd been listening from the corner, nodded slowly. "You've become a teacher, my friend."

"I'm just a sound man."

"We all start somewhere," said Zenko.

# Day 79 — Notes: Teaching What I Know

What I've learned about passing it on.

1. **Start with their question.** Not your lecture.
2. **Show, then explain.** Eyes closed, first.
3. **Keep it short.** Ten minutes beats an hour.
4. **Praise the small thing.** "You caught the lark."
5. **Let them surpass you.** It's the whole point.
6. **Remember someone taught you.** Mine was a quiet old engineer called Mr. Ilko.

If you know something, teach it. To one person. This week.
