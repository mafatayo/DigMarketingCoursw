# Day 17 — Dispatch: A Birthday in the Trench

Bohdan turned twenty-three today and tried very hard to keep it a secret.

He failed because Vira reads everyone's documents. This is a thing she does. She calls it "administrative awareness." Bohdan calls it "being stalked." Three days ago she announced, over the stove, to the entire dugout: "Bohdan's birthday is on Thursday. We're doing something."

He turned the color of beetroot. "Please don't."

"Too late."

Now, resources in a dugout are limited. You can't order a cake. You can't even order a pizza. What you can do is conspire, and conspiracy is something this group does beautifully. For three days the planning happened in whispers, under cover of tea. Zenko was to write a speech. Halyna was in charge of "the cake situation." Motor was observed pretending not to be involved while sanding a small piece of wood for reasons nobody would discuss.

This morning Bohdan went out early to fly a short check, nervous as ever, apologizing to the drone. When he came down the steps, the lamp was lit, the table had a cloth on it, a real cloth, someone's embroidered one, and in the middle sat a cake.

It was a biscuit cake. Crumbled cookies, condensed milk, a little cocoa, pressed into a mess-tin and chilled outside in the cold. On top stood a single candle, a stubby white one, the kind you use in a power cut. Around it Vira had arranged little paper flags cut from the back of a ration box. The cake looked terrible. It was the most beautiful thing I'd seen all month.

We sang. Badly. Zenko was the only one in tune and he refused to be the leader. The Colonel barked at the high notes. Bohdan stood in the doorway with his hands pressed to his face and then, to everyone's horror, started to cry, quietly, with his shoulders going.

"Don't," said Vira, but her voice cracked too.

"I'm just," he said. "I'm just. My mother always makes this one."

Motor, from the corner, held out the wooden thing he'd been sanding. It was a tiny bird, a very rough little swallow, carved flat and smoothed so it fit in a palm. "For your pocket," he said gruffly. "Put it with the other things you carry."

Bohdan took it in both hands. He couldn't speak. He nodded.

Zenko gave his speech. It was short, which surprised everyone. "Twenty-three," he said. "At twenty-three I did nothing of use. You're doing something useful. You're also, I'm told, nice to the drones. That's the whole of a good man. Happy birthday."

We ate the cake in about four minutes. The Colonel got a corner. Bohdan kept the candle, and I saw him later, tucking it carefully into the pocket with the swallow.

# Day 17 — Notes: Small Rituals

A list of things we do that mean more than they look.

- **Touching the doorframe when we go out.** A habit. Nobody remembers who started it.
- **Saying "see you" instead of "goodbye."** Always.
- **Leaving the last mug on the left.** For whoever comes in cold.
- **Reading one line aloud from a letter at breakfast.** A line, not the letter. Just a taste of home for the whole table.
- **Birthday cake, always, of whatever we have.** Even if it's a biscuit crumble.
- **Winding Zenko's old wristwatch every evening.** He asks us to. Nobody knows why. We do it.
- **Tapping the table twice before a meal.** A toast with no words.

**Why they matter:** A ritual is a handle. When everything is slippery, you hold something that's the same today as it was yesterday. It might be foolish. It's also a rope.
