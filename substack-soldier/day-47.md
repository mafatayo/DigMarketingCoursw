*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 47 — Dispatch: A Good Day

I'm going to describe a good day, because I think we need one, and because it's a hard thing to write. Good days leave no marks.

It began with the sun. Not a grand sunrise, but a thin clear one, coming up through a hole in the cloud and lying along the frost like a blade of light. The cold was dry and bright. My breath came out in neat white puffs. The Colonel stood on the sandbags and barked at nothing, with an air of great importance.

Breakfast was eggs. Real ones, from a village woman who'd refused payment. Halyna fried them in a pan on the stove with a little onion, and the smell was so good that Zenko stopped mid-sentence to close his eyes. We ate in silence, each of us in a small private church.

At nine the radio was quiet. Vira put on a song, very low, a woman's voice, old and sweet. She hummed. Motor, at the table, fixed a boot, and Yaroslav sat beside him learning how. Bohdan flew a short check and came back grinning. "Beautiful," he said. "You can see for miles."

At eleven I went out with Zenko to collect water. The walk was cold and pleasant and slow. We talked about nothing. He told me about a student who'd once written an essay about the Cossacks that was, in its entirety, three sentences: *They rode. They fought. They sang.* "Perfect," said Zenko. "I gave him full marks."

At noon the mail came. I got two letters. Katya's was short and silly, about the neighbor's cat opening a window. Sonya's was a drawing of me with enormous hands and my own face, finally, no mustache. "It's you," she'd written, in her giant careful letters. "I know now."

In the afternoon, I slept for an hour on my bunk with the Colonel's chin on my knee, a deep dreamless sleep.

At dusk we sat on the sandbags, six of us in a row, with mugs, while the sky turned violet and the first star came out. Nobody talked. We watched. A bird went by. Somewhere a long way off, something rumbled, and none of us turned our heads.

That was all. That was a good day. No drama, no victory, no big news. Just warm eggs, a clear sky, and the shared quiet of people who like each other.

I wrote it down so I'd remember. If you ever wonder what we're fighting for, I don't think it's flags or maps. I think it's days like that, in as many kitchens as possible, for as long as possible.

# Day 47 — Notes: Defining "Good"

A scale I've developed, rough and unscientific.

**A bad day:** Something hurts that can't be undone.

**An ordinary day:** Nothing goes wrong. Nothing goes particularly right.

**A good day:** One warm meal, one kind word, one letter, one clear hour of sky.

**A great day:** Everyone in the dugout laughs at the same time.

**The best day I've had here:** Today, I think. Which is funny, because nothing happened.

**What it teaches:** You don't need much. You need a few right things in the right order.

What was your last good day, and what was in it?
