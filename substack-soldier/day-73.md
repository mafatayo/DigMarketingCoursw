# Day 73 — Dispatch: Allies and Aid

A pallet arrived with a flag on the side that wasn't ours, and the whole position gathered to look at it.

It was a squared-off block of boxes wrapped in plastic, strapped down, stenciled with a language none of us could read, and a blue-and-yellow sticker slapped on one corner by someone's cheerful hand. Next to it, the driver, who looked exhausted, was filling in a form on a clipboard.

"What is it?" Bohdan whispered.

"Not sure," said Vira. "Let's open it."

It took a while to peel the plastic. When Motor finally cut the straps, the top box fell open, and out came, gloriously, a stack of thermal underwear in neat vacuum-packed rolls, each with a tag. And a handwritten note on top, in careful English: *From the people of a small town you may never have heard of. We think of you. Stay warm.*

"I want to meet them," said Halyna.

Underneath the underwear: medical kits, the proper sort, in sturdy pouches. Boxes of gloves. Dry rations in colored foil. A case of batteries, sturdy ones. A sack of woolen hats.

We spent the afternoon sorting, in the sharp bright chill, with the Colonel inspecting each box. And at some point Zenko, who'd been studying the stenciled language, said, "It's from a country I've never visited, and a place I couldn't point to on a map. And yet here are warm socks."

I'm trying to describe what it does. You'd think aid is about goods, and it is. But the goods are almost the least of it. A pallet from a faraway place carries a quiet, inexhaustible message: *you aren't alone.* A man in a hole at the end of the world looks at a stenciled box in a strange alphabet and realizes that an ocean of people he'll never meet has decided to care.

That matters more than anyone says. In the long winter, loneliness is a more dangerous enemy than the cold.

I also want to be honest. There are moments of frustration. We hear about promises, delays, long debates in distant rooms. Sometimes the things we need most come last. Sometimes the things we need least come first. Bohdan once received eleven crates of a type of battery nothing here uses.

But I try to keep a wider view. Behind each pallet are hundreds of ordinary people, who gave, packed, drove, argued, signed. I'd like to thank them, all of them, from the bottom of the dugout.

Motor took a woolen hat from the sack, turned it over in his big hands, and put it on. It was too small. It sat on top of his head like a cherry on a cake. He regarded us without expression.

"Not a word," he said.

# Day 73 — Notes: What Support Looks Like on the Ground

An unscientific ledger.

**What helps most, in order:**
1. People who show up. Volunteers, drivers, nurses.
2. Steady supplies. Not big, occasional gestures.
3. Honest information. What's coming, and when.
4. Training and tools, for the machines.
5. Solidarity. Flags, letters, voices.
6. Not forgetting.

**What I'd ask the world:**
- Keep going.
- Don't get bored.
- Don't believe the rumors that say we're alone.

If you've given anything at all, thank you. It arrives. It lands in a pair of cold hands.

What's one thing you can keep doing, quietly, month after month?
