# Day 86 — Dispatch: Memory and Monuments

Zenko and I had an argument about statues, and I lost, and I'm glad.

It began at the wall. We'd been looking at the ledger of names, the notebook open on the shelf, and I said, idly, "Someone should build something. A real monument. For all of them."

"What kind?" said Zenko.

"I don't know. A big one. Bronze. Names on it."

"Hmm."

"What?"

"I teach history," he said. "I've seen a great many monuments. And I'll tell you a secret about them." He took off his glasses. "People walk past them without looking."

I laughed. He did not.

"It's true. A statue is a very expensive way of telling a pigeon where to sit. The great ones, the ones that matter, they matter because somebody *makes* you look. By ritual. By ceremony. By telling the story every year to a child. A monument is only as good as the people who stand in front of it and say the names out loud."

"So you don't want one."

"I want *both.*" He put the glasses back on. "I want the bronze, and I want a mother, every spring, bringing a six-year-old to the bronze and saying, 'This man sang while he peeled potatoes.' Without the second, the first is just metal."

I thought of the girl at the green gate. I thought of the pear tree.

"Then what should we remember?" I said. "If we can't remember everything."

"The ordinary things." He was quiet a moment. "I think that's the whole task. We tend to remember the dramatic. A charge. A last stand. A flag. But the dramatic is a tiny fraction. Most of it was a man carrying water. A girl laughing on the radio. A medic washing her hands. It's easy to build a statue of the first. It's much harder to hold the second."

"Then we write it down."

"Yes." He smiled. "You've been doing it all along."

I looked at the notebook. At the fourteen names, then thirty-one, then fifty, in different hands. It had grown past the last page, Halyna had taped in extra sheets, and a few loose cards. It looked less like a record than a small humble quilt.

"Is it enough?" I asked.

"It's a beginning," said Zenko. "Which is all a memorial ever is."

# Day 86 — Notes: What Should Be Remembered

My own list, imperfect.

**Remember the names.** All of them. Even the ones not written down.

**Remember the small things.** The potato song. The cat mug. The four bars.

**Remember the helpers.** The drivers. The cooks. The grandmothers.

**Remember the quiet.** There's more of it than anyone says.

**Remember the cost, but not only the cost.** Remember the joy, too. It was real.

**Remember to tell it.** To a child. In a kitchen. Every year.

What would you want remembered about someone you've lost?
