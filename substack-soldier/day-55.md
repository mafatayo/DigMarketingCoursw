*A work of fiction inspired by the accounts of Ukrainian soldiers.*

# Day 55 — Dispatch: The Ledger of Names

I've been keeping a list.

It started on Day 48, when the name was said at the table and I realized, ashamed, that I didn't have a way to hold it. I'd told myself I'd remember. I will, probably. But memory is a leaky bucket, and I've seen what it does to grief. It wears the edges off. I decided I wasn't going to let it.

So I took the small notebook, the one with the pencil, and turned to the last page. At the top I wrote: *Names.* And under it, the name. Then the date. Then one line about the person.

*Oleksandr. A man who sang while peeling potatoes. Called everyone "friend."*

That was all. A line. It felt like putting down a stone on a path so someone could find it later.

Since then, I've added more. A driver from the convoy who'd shared his thermos. A woman from a village who'd died in her home. A boy I never met, whose name came on the radio. A neighbor of Katya's, from a letter. A teacher Zenko loved. A man from another unit with a very good laugh.

There are fourteen now. I don't talk about the list. But last night Vira found it on my bunk, open, and read it without asking, which I'd normally mind. She stood there in the lamplight for a long minute. Then she took the pencil, very carefully, and wrote one more name at the bottom, in her tidy hand. A friend of hers. A girl from Poltava. "She liked cake," Vira wrote. "She would've liked this idea."

I didn't say anything. I just nodded.

By morning, other additions had appeared. Halyna's handwriting: a name, and *"My first patient, in 2014. He lived. I like to think so."* Motor's block capitals, very small: his brother's. Zenko's elegant script, three names, and a line, *"All of whom I taught."*

It's a ledger now. Not mine. Ours.

I don't think any of us expected it. I certainly didn't. But there's a kind of quiet, communal hush around that notebook now. It sits on the shelf next to the cat mug, and people stop beside it on the way to the stove. Nobody opens it, mostly. But they know it's there. They know the names are held.

I've decided to carry it with me. When I go home, I'll take it. I'll read every name to Sonya when she's old enough, and tell her how each of them was kind.

# Day 55 — Notes: How We Remember

Some of the ways, and what I think about them.

**Names.** On a page, a wall, a stone. A name is a small unbreakable thing.

**Objects.** A mug, a pocketknife, a hat. They hold fingerprints.

**Stories.** The best. Told at tables. Told badly.

**Songs.** Zenko sings one now, for the first of them.

**Rituals.** A glass poured and not drunk. A candle. A silence.

**Photographs.** Even a bad one. Especially a bad one.

**What I won't do:** Make anyone a symbol. They were people. They had bad moods and favorite socks.

If you could keep one thing of someone you've lost, what would it be?
