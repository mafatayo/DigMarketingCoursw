# Day 51 — Dispatch: Telling the Truth to Family

Katya asked me a direct question last night, and I lied.

It was a good lie. Warm, practical, well-constructed. I've been practicing for weeks. She said, over a poor line, with the crackling hiss of distance in her voice, "Danylo, how is it really?"

And I said, "It's fine. It's quiet. We're just waiting."

A pause. Long. The line hummed. I could hear the little domestic sounds of our kitchen behind her, the tap that doesn't drip anymore, a pan on the stove, Sonya singing something tuneless in the next room.

"You're lying," she said, gently.

"No."

"You've lost weight in your voice."

I laughed, then stopped. "Katya."

"I know you can't tell me things. I'm not asking for things. I'm asking if you're all right."

I stood at the post, in the cold, with the phone against my cheek and my breath fogging. I wanted so badly to tell her. About the bad day, the name I said at the table, the empty chair in my head. About the smell of fear and the dog on my chest and the way my hands shook at the stretcher. I wanted to put every bit of it down on the line and let it run to her kitchen and be shared.

I didn't. I want you to understand why, because I think it's the most common mistake we make.

If I tell her, she'll carry it. She'll carry it into the night, to the bakery, to Sonya's school gate, to her mother's table. She'll hold it for me, because she loves me, and she'll never put it down. I'd be handing her a stone to hold across four hundred kilometers, and she couldn't do a single thing about it.

So I gave her a smaller true thing.

"I'm tired," I said. "More than I've ever been. I'm frightened sometimes. But I'm with good people. Really good. They look after me. I've got a dog and a mug and a friend who reads dragon stories. I'm all right, Katya. Not fine. All right."

She was silent. Then, very softly: "Thank you."

"For what?"

"For giving me the real size of it."

We stayed on the line a little longer without talking. I listened to her breathe. In the next room Sonya had stopped singing. I thought I heard her say "Mama?" and the line broke up.

I stood a long while with the dead phone against my ear.

# Day 51 — Notes: What I Don't Say on the Phone

Not lies, exactly. Edits. A rough list.

**I don't say:**
- The names of places.
- The names of the hurt.
- How close it got.
- What the nights are like.
- That I've written a letter "in case."

**I do say:**
- That I ate.
- That I'm warm.
- That the dog likes me.
- That I think of them, hourly.
- That I'm learning something.
- That I love them. Every call, once, plainly.

**What I'm learning:** There's a difference between lying and carrying. Between hiding and protecting. I'm trying to stay on the right side of it. I'm not always sure I do.

Do you shield the people you love from the hard parts? Should you?
