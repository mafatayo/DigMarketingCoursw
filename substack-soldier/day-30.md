# Day 30 — Dispatch: Day Thirty

One month. Nobody made a fuss, which I appreciated. Motor didn't announce it. Vira didn't bake anything. But at breakfast Halyna set an extra spoon of sugar in my tea, slid it across the table without a word, and I understood the whole thing.

I went up on the sandbags this morning with the notebook. It was cold, clean, bright, the sort of morning where every blade of grass has its own tiny frost coat and the whole field looks as if someone has dusted it with salt. The sun came up pale yellow behind a line of bare trees. The Colonel stood beside me, breathing steam.

I tried to think what to say about thirty days. What came was a list, so I made a list.

I have learned the names of birds I used to call "birds."

I can sleep sitting up.

I can eat anything hot and lie about the flavor.

I can tell Motor's moods by how he hangs a towel.

I've said goodbye to people I didn't know existed a month ago. Not forever. Just: the supply guys, a man from another unit who had a very good laugh, a woman driver who brought us three crates of cabbage and wouldn't take a thank-you. They're all faces I'll carry.

I've been scared. I've been bored. I've been cold. I've been happier than I expected, in quick, strange bursts, and then felt guilty about it, and then forgiven myself, usually over tea.

I miss Katya so much it sits in my teeth. I miss Sonya's voice on the phone, a small high squeak saying, "Papa, Papa, guess what," followed by an enormous silence in which she's clearly forgotten.

Yet there's something else, harder to name. A kind of clarity. For thirty days I haven't worried about my email. Or my rent. Or whether I'd said the clever thing. The problems have shrunk to their true size: Is there water? Is there a dry sock? Is my friend okay? It's both terrible and clean.

Zenko came up and stood beside me, wrapped up in both scarves. "A month," he said.

"A month."

"You've done well, gentlemen."

"Don't call me gentlemen."

"A habit." He looked at the field for a while. "Do you know what Plutarch says about courage?"

"No."

"Neither do I. I made it up. But it sounded good."

I laughed. A real laugh, big. It rolled out over the frost.

We went down for tea.

# Day 30 — Notes: Who I Was Thirty Days Ago

A letter to the man at the bus stop.

Dear Danylo, with the bag,

You're about to walk past a bakery and think the smell will follow you. It will. You'll keep it. It becomes a small lamp.

You think you're not brave. You're right. Don't worry about it. Courage here isn't what you imagined. It's carrying water. It's saying "I'll take the next watch." It's folding a letter in half so someone else can read the good part.

You'll be afraid. A lot. It won't go away. You'll learn to walk beside it, like a dog.

You'll meet people you'd never choose, and you'll be glad they're yours.

You'll eat pickles from a woman you've never seen. You'll get a letter from an eight-year-old. You'll hold a mug with a cartoon cat on it and feel like a king.

You'll find out that you can sleep on a plank, wash in a bucket, and laugh at three in the morning.

You'll still be you. Just thinner.

Call Katya more. She's braver than you.

— Lys
