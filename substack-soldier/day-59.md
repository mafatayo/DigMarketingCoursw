# Day 59 — Dispatch: Hospital Visit

I went to see Bohdan's friend in the hospital, and I'd like to describe the smell of the corridor before anything else, because it's the one thing I'll never forget.

Disinfectant, boiled cabbage, wet wool, and underneath, a faint sweetness that I won't name. It's the smell of people being put back together. A long clean corridor, painted a pale uncertain green, with a row of plastic chairs against one wall and, at the end, an old woman mopping the floor in slow, even sweeps, like a priest.

We'd been given a day's pass to the rear, a small miracle of Motor's persuasion. A truck took us part of the way. We carried a bag of oranges, a few letters, a deck of cards, and Yaroslav's mother's spare scarf, which he insisted on donating.

The friend's name was Dmytro. Twenty-four. He had been in Bohdan's year at university and they'd shared a room, a kettle, and a deeply stupid running joke about a three-legged chair. He'd been wounded some weeks before. I won't say how. He was in the fourth bed on the left, near a window that looked onto a courtyard of bare linden trees.

I'd expected, I think, a pale, silent man bound in white. What we got was Dmytro sitting up against a heap of pillows, one leg in a frame, a book open on his chest, and an expression of vast irritation.

"You're late," he said.

"Traffic," said Bohdan.

"You look terrible."

"You look worse."

"I look *brilliant.* I have a nurse who brings me jelly."

They stared at each other for a long second. Then Bohdan sat heavily on the edge of the bed, and Dmytro put an arm round his neck and pulled him in, forehead to forehead. They stayed like that for a minute. I turned and looked out the window at the lindens, to give them the room.

We stayed three hours. We played cards, badly, because Dmytro cheated and we let him. We gave him the oranges, which he peeled with extraordinary ceremony. Zenko, who'd come too, told him a long and improbable story about a general and a goose. Dmytro laughed so hard that a nurse came in and told us to go easy.

When it was time to leave, he caught my sleeve. "You're the sound man," he said. "Bohdan talks about you."

"What does he say?"

"That you listen like it matters."

I didn't know what to say.

"It does," said Dmytro.

# Day 59 — Notes: Recovery Is a Job

A few things I learned from the ward.

1. **Recovery is slow.** The body takes its time, and so does the mind.
2. **It's work.** Exercises, pain, patience, setbacks.
3. **Visitors help.** Bring jokes. Bring cards. Bring oranges.
4. **Don't pity.** Ask. "What do you need?" Then listen.
5. **The nurses are saints.** Treat them as such.
6. **Humor survives.** Often, defiantly.
7. **Everyone's terrified of going home.** Not of the pain. Of being a burden. They aren't.

**If you can:** Visit someone in hospital this week. Anyone. It matters more than the oranges.
