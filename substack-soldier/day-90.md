# Day 90 — Dispatch: Day Ninety

It's early. The kettle's on. There's a thin silver light in the east, and a lark has already begun, going up and up into a sky the color of the inside of a shell.

I'm on the sandbags with the notebook on my knee. The Colonel is beside me, gray at the muzzle now, or maybe I'm only noticing. His head is on my boot. Behind me, through the open door, I can hear the small sounds of the dugout waking: Motor's cough, Vira muttering something about the radio, a spoon on a mug. A lamp clicks on. Somebody laughs.

I want to tell you what I thought I'd say here. I'd imagined a big ending. A summing-up. A line you could underline. I've been composing it for weeks, in the dark, on watch. Something about the war, or the future, or what it all meant.

I find I don't have one. I have a kettle, and a dog, and a list of names. I have a rabbit in my breast pocket and a small wooden fox from a sergeant. I have a tin of letters to the future, taped with pink. I have a family I'd never have chosen and wouldn't trade.

Ninety days ago I stood in a kitchen with a cup of tea I didn't want and a wife who put a second cup beside mine. I thought I was walking toward something terrible. I was. But I was also walking toward Motor's four bars, and Halyna's warm hands, and Vira's slammed-door laugh, and a mug with a cartoon cat.

I've been more afraid than I've ever been. I've also been more loved. I didn't expect the second.

Here's what I know. It isn't over. I'm not going to tell you it is. There's more cold coming, and more mud, and more letters I'll be afraid to open. People I love will be tired and people I haven't met yet will need tea. The war doesn't end because a count reaches ninety.

But I'm here, and so are you. Thank you for that. You've been the stranger with the letter, the grandmother with the pickles, the girl with the blue paper heart. You've been the woman who drove seven hours. You've been, I'd like to think, the bakery smell on the morning I left.

Motor has come up the steps. He stands beside me, hands in his pockets, looking east. He doesn't say anything. After a while he starts to hum.

Four bars. Up, up, down. A long note that doesn't resolve.

And then, to my astonishment, he doesn't stop at four. He keeps going. A fifth. A sixth. The whole tune, low and rough, a woman at the end of a long road, an evening coming, a thermos.

I don't look at him. I don't dare. I just listen.

# Day 90 — Notes: Not an Ending

There isn't a list today. Only this.

If you take one thing from ninety days:

*Put the kettle on. Fill it to the line. Whoever empties it fills it. Look after the person next to you. Say the names. Listen under the first sound.*

*Be gentle. It's very easy. It's very rare.*

I'll keep writing, as long as there's something worth saying and a pencil. Come back. Tell me about your Tuesday.

*Dyakuyu.*

*— Lys*
