# Day 57 — Dispatch: Boxes from Donors

A truck of donations arrived today, and Motor opened it the way a man opens a letter from the tax office.

"Check the labels," he said. "I want to know what each box is before we touch it."

It's a sensible policy. Not every donation helps. Over the weeks we've received some astonishing things. A crate of fine porcelain teacups. Seventeen pairs of ice skates. A box of ties. A large framed painting of a lake, with a note: *For morale.* A small, kind, bewildering sack of confetti.

So we approached this latest load warily, lined up like customs officers. Vira read the labels out loud. "Batteries... bandages... wool socks... thermal underwear..."

"Oh, thank God," said Bohdan.

"...a bag of oranges... duct tape, forty rolls..."

"Forty!"

"...children's letters... a harmonica..."

We went through it box by box, a long delighted afternoon. By dusk our little dugout looked like a general store. Halyna had a stack of bandages she was handling like a bank robber. Bohdan sat on a crate with a huge box of electronics and the face of a child at the end of a very good holiday. Zenko was reading every child's letter aloud, in a trembling voice.

I found, at the bottom of one crate, a note from the people who'd packed it. Eight volunteers, in a hall in a city I'll never see, working at long tables, with a handwritten inventory. They'd sorted the socks by size. They'd put a tangerine in each box. A woman had drawn a small smiling sun on the lid in marker, and signed it *Nadia.*

I thought about her. Nadia, bending over a table, thinking of a stranger who'd wear these. She'd never know us. She'd never know Bohdan wept over the batteries. She'd never see Motor, a big man, hold a pair of wool socks to his face and close his eyes.

That's the thing. You can't see the whole chain. You see the boxes, and the dust on them, and the writing. Somewhere behind each one is a kitchen table, a woman, a Tuesday evening spent folding clothes for people far away.

"We should write back," said Halyna.

"To who?"

"All of them."

We didn't, of course. We couldn't. But I wrote one note, for the next box heading back down the line, and I taped it to the lid. It said only: *To whoever packed this: you were seen.*

# Day 57 — Notes: Where Donations Actually Go

Honest tips from the receiving end. Take them as you like.

**What's useful, nearly always:**
- Warm clothes, wool, thermal.
- Medical supplies, especially tourniquets and bandages.
- Batteries, power banks, chargers.
- Tape, tools, zip ties.
- Hygiene items: soap, wipes, foot powder.
- Non-perishable food.
- Letters. Always.

**What's less useful:**
- Fine china.
- Anything fragile.
- Anything that needs a dry room.
- Clothes in the wrong size, unlabeled.
- Large items with no way to carry.

**How to give well:**
1. Ask a reliable volunteer group what's needed.
2. Send what's asked for.
3. Label everything.
4. Keep receipts. Trust is built on paper.
5. Beware of anyone promising miracles.
6. Give regularly. Small and steady beats big and once.

And if you can't give money, write a note. We keep every one.
