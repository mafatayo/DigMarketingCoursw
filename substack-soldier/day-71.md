# Day 71 — Dispatch: What We Argue About

Not politics. I take it back. We argue about plenty, and almost none of it is the big stuff.

Here is a partial list of the arguments this week.

**The kettle.** Whether it should be filled to the line or to the brim. Halyna is a brim person. Vira is a line person. It has become a cold war, fought with small passive-aggressive adjustments to water levels. Motor, asked to arbitrate, said, "I fix engines, not marriages."

**The order of the pickle jars.** Bohdan likes them alphabetical. There are three jars.

**Whether a hedgehog counts as a pet.** Yaroslav says yes. The Colonel, looking at the hedgehog through the doorway, appears to disagree.

**How to pronounce a particular word in a particular song.** An argument that lasted from breakfast until Tuesday and ended with Zenko leaving the room with great dignity.

**Whether Vira is "a morning person."** She is not. She says she is. We all know.

And beneath all that, once or twice a week, a bigger one. Strategy. What the army should do, what the commanders are thinking, whether the plan is wise, whether the numbers add up. Those arguments have a different flavor, sharper, closer to the bone. Men who've held a line for months have opinions about the line, and they're entitled to them.

I'll tell you what's remarkable. Nobody here has ever failed to do a job because of an argument. We can shout at midnight about the plan and walk out at dawn to carry water for the same plan. I think it's because the argument isn't about winning. It's about being heard.

"I disagree with you completely," Motor told Bohdan last night, after forty minutes of heat. "And I'd give you my last sock."

"I know," said Bohdan. "I'd give you both of mine."

"You have holes in them."

"Then you'll have *ventilation.*"

Even Motor laughed.

I think there's something here worth bottling. People who'd rather die than be unkind to each other, who disagree on everything but care. If a whole country could do that, argue with a sock in hand, I think it'd be unbeatable.

I've started, quietly, to keep a tally of the things we agree on. It's long. It includes: tea, dogs, dry feet, mothers, the importance of dill, the correct way to boil buckwheat, and the fact that the kettle should be filled *exactly* to the line, and that Halyna is wrong.

# Day 71 — Notes: Strategy vs. Survival

An honest distinction.

**Strategy is what the planners do.** It's maps, numbers, the long view.

**Survival is what we do.** It's socks, water, sleep, a watch.

**They need each other.** A good plan fails with cold feet. Warm feet fail with no plan.

**What the front sees that the map doesn't:**
- The mood of the men.
- The state of the mud.
- The exact weight of a stretcher.

**What the map sees that the front doesn't:**
- The shape of the whole.
- The thing behind the hill.

**What I wish:** That they'd listen to each other more. That a general would sometimes carry water. That a soldier would sometimes read a map.

Where have you seen the big view and the small view fail to meet?
