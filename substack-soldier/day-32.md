# Day 32 — Dispatch: The Workshop

I should explain the corner.

In the back of the dugout, behind a hanging blanket, there's a bench. It's a door laid across two crates, and on it, in a scatter of precise chaos, lives Bohdan's workshop. A soldering iron with a burnt handle. A coil of thin wire. Tiny screwdrivers in a mug. Zip ties, tape in six colors, a few little circuit boards with solder blobs like silver tears. A magnifying glass held by an old desk lamp. A jar of screws sorted by size, labeled in his neat student handwriting: *M2, M2.5, "mystery."*

He sits there for hours with his tongue between his teeth, bent over a motor the size of a thumb, humming something that isn't a song.

I'd never really watched him work. Today I did. The first thing I noticed was how different he is. The twitchy, apologetic kid disappears. His hands go still. His eyes sharpen. When a tiny wire needs to be placed, he places it with a patience that makes Halyna's hands look rough.

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Fixing a motor. The bearing's worn. It sounds like a coffee grinder."

"Can you fix it?"

"I can make it limp for another ten flights." He glanced up with a flicker of pride. "Better than buying new. New takes a month."

I watched him work for an hour. It's a strange, tender craft. A drone arrives bruised, with a bent arm, a cracked frame, a motor that won't turn. He opens it like a surgeon. He talks to it. "Okay. Okay, good girl, I see. Right, that's the problem." He replaces a part with a bit of something from another dead drone. He patches a frame with plastic cut from a water bottle and a zip tie. Sometimes with a piece of a toy a child sent in a parcel. The little yellow wheel of a broken toy truck is now, as far as I know, helping a quadcopter stay level.

"Where do you learn this?" I asked.

"Videos. Forums. My own mistakes." He laughed. "I broke eight before I understood one."

I asked him if he ever felt strange about the work. He took a while to answer.

"I think about it," he said finally. "But I think about the guys on the ground. What I can do is keep them from walking into something blind. Sometimes it's that simple. I look, so they don't have to." He picked up the little motor, turned it in the lamplight. "The machine doesn't think. I do. I try to think well."

He went back to his bench. After a while I heard it start up again. Not the whine of a flight. Just a motor, repaired, spinning softly on the table, steady and small and alive.

# Day 32 — Notes: Soldering Irons and Duct Tape

The unofficial list of what keeps a small workshop alive.

- **Duct tape.** Obviously. Gray, black, and a roll of pink from a donor. The pink holds just as well and cheers everyone up.
- **Zip ties.** A thousand uses.
- **Old phone batteries.** Don't ask.
- **A good magnifying glass.** Treasure.
- **Patience.** The rarest resource. Bohdan has a storage of it that I envy.
- **A cup of tea, set at arm's length.** Never closer. He has ruined one circuit board with it already.
- **Spare parts from fallen machines.** Even a dead drone gives something back.
- **Kids' toys.** Wheels, screws, tiny rubber bands. We keep a box.

**What impresses me:** The whole thing is improvised. And yet it works. This war runs on ingenuity as much as anything. There are small workshops like this one in dugouts all along the line, run by students, mechanics, hobbyists.

I used to think "technology" meant something clean and expensive. Here it's solder fumes and cold fingers.
